# Tabletop wind-tunnel impulse transfer

Gamella and colleagues demonstrate an exhaust-speed response to an intake intervention in Figure 7. Here the quantitative question is whether the excess torque waveform estimated at middle exhaust duty can predict exhaust speed and downwind pressure at two different operating points. The unchanging exhaust command, a rotor with no cross-fan torque term, and the finite-difference torque estimate give distinct predictions.

The 11-arm, two-session test failed its joint criterion. The interval-calibrated model gave the lowest exhaust-speed RMSE at low duty, 66.989 RPM. At high duty the simple hatch/time template won on both speed and pressure. Solving the numerical model accurately did not make the inferred forcing transport across both recorded operating points.

## What the chamber recorded

The protocol fixes each exhaust-duty/pressure-oversampling combination for a full acquisition. Its generator waits 7 s at session start, then assigns a hatch setting, waits 500 ms, measures five baseline rows, sets intake duty to one for 20 measured rows and restores 0.01 for 25. These are row counts, not fixed elapsed durations. All 5,000 flags in each of the five acquisitions set the recorder's first-after-command indicator at rows 0, 5 and 25 and nowhere else. Each session has 249,997 counter increments of one and two wraps of −100,000. The longest interval inside a pulse reaches 1.539 s in the low-duty and 1.646 s in the high-duty session. The release labels the measurement timestamp with an accuracy of a few milliseconds. It does not provide a synchronized continuous trace of the actual motor command, a separate time for each instrument or a calibrated delay from airflow to recorded pressure. Tachometer setting 1 times turns with a microsecond clock. A stopped, unpowered fan can retain the last tachometer reading. Oversampling 4 averages multiple pressure readings. All three scored or training impulse sessions use the same oversampling level. The source warns of manufacturing offsets between barometers. We predict one named downwind instrument conditional on its own starting measurement.

Each session has 250,000 recorded rows in 5,000 consecutive flags. Acquisition timestamps increase throughout each file. Across the five sessions, their median row intervals are approximately 151.56 ms, including event boundaries. The protocol draws hatch settings from a uniform distribution with a single seed 7 and rounds commands to 0.1 degree. This gives many responses within one exhaust setting. Each setting occurs in only one acquisition, and the different settings run in sequence. Differences across sessions mix duty with acquisition period. No between-session standard error or independent mechanical-mediation estimate follows. The middle-duty recording at OSR4 begins first, the high-duty session follows, then two other middle-duty sensor settings and finally the low-duty session.

The released random-walk generator supplies 16 seeds. The archive's first seed has 1,016 rows and the others have 10,000. Additive and interaction controls use identical complete 50-row blocks, features centered by their first five rows and targets defined as changes from that same prefix. Seed 16 is reserved for development. Training uses 20 complete windows from the short first run, with its final 16 rows documented and unused. Inputs enter both additive response equations, so this control already allows intake to affect exhaust RPM. The released uncoupled rotor equation and persistence are separate comparisons.

## Parameter sources and code path

The parameter notebook converts current digitizer values with a calibration from the distinct analog-calibration and no-load recordings. It uses a reference voltage of 1.16 V, minimum motor current 0.166 A, maximum 0.27 A and minimum nonzero effective duty 0.1. The appendix gives nominal maximum current as 0.26 A; this replay uses the executable notebook's 0.27 A. Rotor maximum speed is 3,000 RPM. Inertia is approximated with a disk of radius 0.059 m and mass 0.02 kg. Torque constant is 0.05 Nm/A and drag is inferred at maximum speed. These are the author's approximations, not a direct torque calibration of the held apparatus. The two rotors integrate separately with 100 right-endpoint Euler substeps over each measured interval.

The pressure law intersects the fan pressure–flow characteristic with a quadratic airflow impedance. It uses maximum flow 186.7 m³/h, maximum static pressure 74.8247395 Pa and hatch-dependent flow ratio 0.75 + 0.15 H/45. The paper's separate Bernoulli pressure-offset experiment concerns a different model. The A2+C3 notebook calls the pressure simulator with the first observed downwind pressure as its ambient anchor, a zero barometer error, Gaussian standard deviation 0.2 Pa and seed 3. We restart that explicit benchmark on each 50-row pulse. Both new empirical controls and the centered author comparator instead anchor on the common five-sample mean. Exact published model and simulator files are vendored under their MIT headers, and the source branch pins the generating notebook and original datasets.

A fixed pressure anchor and fan laws cannot separate electrical-supply interactions, tunnel geometry, ambient drift, rotor coupling and instrument lag. The measured speed trajectory can support a test of transport between settings even when these physical causes are not individually identifiable.

## Development evidence

On the 1,000 middle-duty development events, torque transfer produced RMSE 9.7401 RPM and 0.74567 Pa. The hatch/time template gave 10.0565 RPM and 0.74259 Pa. The two-fan regression plus pulse-template residual gave 14.2032 RPM and 0.71606 Pa. In the calibration data, flag 422 at within-event row 26 rose 243.10 RPM above its own five-row baseline. Pressure at flag 676 row 25 rose 19.83 Pa. These numbers inform the choice of controls. Two authentic training pulses reproduce the published simulator's exhaust speed exactly at the stored precision. The largest absolute pressure difference at double precision was 1.46 × 10⁻¹¹ Pa. The initial two-holdout protocol required all eight controls to lose on both units separately on each acquisition.

The executable notebook's arithmetic subtracts the no-load current after adding the cubic current increment. A separate evaluation of this literal function definition on the two source flags reproduces all three stored float32 series, including intake RPM. This check concerns the published parameters and the adapter. It supplies no accuracy estimate for a held pulse.

## Numerical successor frozen before held predictions

The first candidate inferred a row-indexed drive from source mean time intervals and applied a single explicit Euler update per measured target interval. That discretization changed predictions enough to matter. On the 1,000 middle-duty development events, the one-step results differed from exact integration of the same drive by 2.4781 RPM RMS. The largest difference was 31.316 RPM at flag 4782, row 15, where the interval was 1.2165 s. Splitting every interval into 4, 16 and 64 steps reduced the RMS differences to 0.5652, 0.1391 and 0.0347 RPM. No zero clamp activated. Applying the same calculation to the held command clocks with fixed nominal starting speeds of 300 and 3,000 RPM yielded maximum one-step discrepancies of 23.932 and 51.789 RPM. Those checks selected no held measured speed or pressure.

The successor infers one constant drive for each of the 45 response intervals in the source template. The source waveform, hatch-bin profile and interval lengths are estimated from the same middle-duty training trials. These estimates do not provide an independently measured continuous torque trace. For a constant drive and positive quadratic drag, the scalar rotor equation has analytic positive-drive, zero-drive and negative-drive solutions. With negative drive a physical rotor reaching zero stays at rest through the interval. The average squared speed between two positive source endpoints lies between their smaller and larger squared speeds. This bounds the constant drive uniquely and supplies the bisection bracket in `torque_flow.py`.

The first inferred interval starts from the declared five-row mean. The learned row-four speed differs from that mean by as much as 3.3251 RPM. Over 18,045 parameterized source intervals spanning 401 distinct recorded training hatch positions, the maximum complete-trajectory reconstruction error was 3.53 × 10⁻¹² RPM. Thirteen separate tests cover either sign of the applied drive, a stopped rotor, equilibrium, a near-equilibrium update and a zero-length interval. The analytic flow agrees with event-stopped DOP853 to 1.97 × 10⁻¹² rad/s and composes across divided intervals to 2.85 × 10⁻¹⁴ rad/s. True physical forcing may vary within each interval.

Both the original one-step candidate and exact forward integration of its original drive remain as controls. The new candidate uses the interval inverse and exact target propagation together. On middle-duty development it scored 9.64755 RPM and 0.74471 Pa, compared with 9.83334 RPM and 0.74450 Pa for the original drive with exact forward integration. The original and successor experiments are separately identified in Git. The expanded held test locks 11 functions across two sessions and requires the successor to beat all ten controls at each of four endpoints. None of the original refused forecast requests produced held arrays.

The two immutable forecast archives occupy 12,889,547 and 10,532,342 bytes. The native producer's original archives were copied to content-addressed numerical evidence storage and both copies were read back completely and verified by SHA-256. `numerical-custody.json` identifies them, the original worker/process receipts and the recorded pre-dispatch refusals.
