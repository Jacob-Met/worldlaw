# Executable comparator and its scientific boundary

The 2017 Bauza–Rodriguez study fits three separate heteroscedastic Gaussian processes for push-frame translation and rotation, by material and object, using the earlier general-purpose collection. Its published square/plywood illustration predicts motion over 0.2 s, equivalent to 4 mm of the commanded 20 mm/s motion. The repeated collection's full commanded push is 10 mm, equivalent to 0.5 s. The authors evaluate normalized mean squared error and the log probability from the product of the separate output densities. A future full-push score and a source-conditioned prefix forecast need separate labels and cannot inherit the historical published score.

The primary algorithm authors’ MATLAB implementation in IPL-UV/simpleR revision ced8e7b0063fee3a921e65c048f9df893e61a4ee, VHGPR/vhgpr.m (Git blob 866b7bc3295b8ca4ba6f4724f374c1d9942d27f2), is available. GNU Octave 8.4 executed 18 immutable MATLAB files on 24 deterministic synthetic training points and 3 synthetic queries. Changing all three test labels preserved all posterior means and variances exactly while both scores changed. The entire native output is in vhgpr-native-original.txt. This authentic algorithm run supplies neither the pushing authors’ learned checkpoint nor reproduction of their historical experiment.

The released UI initializes an ARD length scale by half the training range for each input. With a single commanded speed of 20 mm/s, this range is zero and the log initializer is singular. In a future comparison limited to the repeated archive's fixed-speed design, omit this constant feature from all input-dependent learners; fit the source-only action model on position and angle. A fit across genuine alternative speeds would be a different information budget. Retain any refused or unstable physical run and never add test-selected jitter or tune a fitted prior from held outcomes.

The conditional ellipsoidal model takes a compressive force on the object at planar point r. Its local twist is proportional to (Fx,Fy,(rx Fy−ry Fx)/c²) for a positive torsional radius c. Constant-direction scaling is fixed only when the measured pusher normal velocity and the normal velocity induced at r have compatible signs and a nonsingular ratio. Multiplying the force by a common positive factor leaves the local prediction unchanged. The tested code conditional_contact.py checks centered and off-center contact, exact positive-rescaling invariance, compatibility with the nonpenetration equation, and rejection of tensile force. Source-only numerical qualification does not establish whether the physical record contains the required point-contact and quasistatic regime. In particular the sensor measures force on the pusher; its signed torque about the sensor origin cannot be substituted for torque about the object center without the appropriate force and moment transport.
