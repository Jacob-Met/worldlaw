# Predicting the opposite-end reaction of a bowed cello string

A causal point-force string model with a source-calibrated filter predicted all six complete nut-force traces better when supplied the recorded bow position. Mean per-stroke RMSE was **0.2530 N** at the new position, compared with **0.2920 N** for the identical filter at the old position, **0.3157 N** for the strongest empirical FIR and **0.4276 N** for a calibrated signed half-period shift. The preregistered gate passed. All six strokes come from one neighboring position, one specimen and one recording folder. Errors remain large at individual samples.

## What the instruments observe

Alessio Lampis and colleagues recorded a robotic bow acting on a cello G2 string mounted on a Vienna monochord. The released four-column records hold bow force, bow speed and two string-end reactions in the 50 kHz acquisition. The bridge uses strain-gauge load cells with static and dynamic response. The nut uses piezoelectric ceramics with a charge amplifier and cannot sense a static force. In the authors’ separate centered wire-break check, the Newton-calibrated nut signal and high-passed bridge signal agree in their first pluck cycles. This qualifies an AC comparison on this apparatus. It does not certify the phase, gain or rigidity of every mounting. [Dataset, version 17749111](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.17749111) · [Instrument technical report, §2.1](https://doi.org/10.21939/iwk-tech-report-1-2024) · [Measurement study](https://caml.music.mcgill.ca/lib/exe/fetch.php?media=publications%3Alampis_ismra2025.pdf)

The first 56 catalogued strokes from `2024-03-27_r_v1` comprise 50 source-position strokes near β=0.2001 and six at β≈0.1886, where β is the bow-to-bridge distance divided by the string length. The source median on the 40 fitted strokes was 0.20009294. Strokes 5, 10, …, 50 served solely for model selection. Strokes 51–56 were held in their entirety. Initial source strokes reached 0.05 m/s on the released speed channel. The general deposit description maps folder 1 to 0.1 m/s; the file path and actual channel readings take precedence here. Acquisition order does not establish a commanded force. Position changes coincide with a later sequence of strokes and may coincide with retuning, string settling, or changes in the bow contact.

## Why harmonic magnitudes do not identify position

For a flexible uniform string with rigid ends, a transverse point force a distance a from the bridge produces a signed nut-to-bridge transfer sin(ka)/sin(k(L−a)). This follows by matching displacement on the two string segments and differentiating at their supports. At undamped whole-string resonances kL=nπ, the ratio at nonnodal contacts reduces to (−1)^(n+1), independently of a. Thus the signed half-period bridge shift is a necessary comparison. Conversely, at a zero of the bridge observation, the nut can still have a finite reaction. At β≈0.2001 and nominal fundamental 98 Hz, the first such antiresonance is near 122.5 Hz. No stable, unconditional steady-state inverse follows from the two instruments.

We evaluated a regularized, finite-time causal prediction from the measured bridge trace. A disturbance travels across a fraction of the string in the corresponding fraction of its one-way transit time. The impulse response has a first delay proportional to 1−2β, a subtractive echo separated by 2β, and further round trips separated by 2(1−β). Each term uses the candidate attenuation raised to the corresponding path length. The kernel spans the entire available record from a cold start. Fractional arrivals use linear interpolation at 50 kHz. Independent integer-delay synthetic tests reconstruct the driven nut response through the bridge and directly from the same applied force.

The 31-tap causal output filter learns a common signed calibration from source strokes. Source validation chose 98 Hz and one-way retention 0.85. The scalar-only fitted gain was −0.930 and the half-period arm learned −0.473 multiplying its already signed input. These coefficients describe the observed channels under these fits. They do not establish force polarity, bow friction, or physical attenuation. Finite bow width, bending dispersion, support motion and sensor response can all contribute. The matched frozen-geometry control uses the same selected calibration and changes only the β supplied to the kernel.

## Source evidence and the held lock

The first three designated pilot strokes had causal 100 ms onset bridge RMS of 0.438, 0.442 and 0.335 N. Their first-50-ms baselines were 0.0050, 0.0045 and 0.0043 N. Fractions 0.872, 0.825 and 0.624 of band-limited source energy lay outside ±8 Hz neighborhoods of the nominal 98 Hz harmonics. Pitch change, nonperiodicity and measurement effects remain possible. On stroke 1 the measured filtered nut reached +2.776 N while its individually scaled half-period diagnostic gave +0.00175 N. On the ten separate source-development strokes, the 241-tap FIR averaged 0.0447 N RMSE and the calibrated mechanics model 0.0511 N.

The split and prerequisite were published at `3eb3f6377b15cc69253a5f281d84ba63771dbb7e`. The complete predictor, receiver and acceptance rule were frozen at `2c22c42f28e9c098ea8fd0e31a30585feb394928`. All 42 float32 forecast vectors and the fitted calibration were then locked at `6bfb0b01cdf77e5c5e5e2a7612ffcda1e610b720` before the held receiver selected nut readings. The predictor verified complete interleaved member bytes but numerically selected only bridge force and bow velocity in the held recordings. The velocity channel serves solely to locate the secondary onset.

Both channels use the same causal fourth-order Butterworth bandpass with cutoffs 25 and 800 Hz before 10-to-1 decimation. The first same-channel sample sets the baseline within each recording. Whole-record scores start at sample zero. The secondary 100 ms onset begins at the first ten sustained milliseconds with absolute recorded speed at least 0.02 m/s. Smaller mean error on six new-position strokes does not establish reliable peak reconstruction or distinguish the physical source of this error.

## Custody and replay

The versioned Zenodo Type A archive has 14,083,546,513 compressed bytes and one solid LZMA2 block. Its directory describes 18,000 files over 74,179,785,108 decoded bytes. Only 144 MiB of the compressed data stream were needed here. The intake verified HTTP byte ranges, 7z header checksums, complete selected member CRCs, each selected whole-stroke SHA-256, and separate SHA-256 values for the retained 128 MiB prefix and 16 MiB suffix. The archived publisher MD5 applies to the whole 14.08 GB file and was not independently recomputed. An initial directory observer failed on py7zr’s named-file convenience API. A later index assumption failed after the first 50 pairs because timestamp members intervene. The corrected receiver used genuine catalog order and recovered only the missing suffix. Recording structure, data access stages and all native originals are documented here.

Run `python recount.py .` for an offline independent recount from the locked arrays. It checks all 1,062,642 finite sample residuals, 399 scalar comparisons, the 42 role/stroke pairs, seven exact worst indices, and the pass/fail logic. The largest numerical difference was 1.11×10⁻¹⁶ N. Running `python source_ranges.py INPUT_DIR` recovers the two authenticated public source segments. With scipy installed, `python raw_worst_receive.py INPUT_DIR score.json` independently checks the source member, seven raw CSV rows, and the worst filtered target by scalar recursion using the same Butterworth coefficients. The reported seven filtered values have zero difference on the original receiver. The original fit used numpy 2.2.6 and scipy 1.15.3. Re-fitting is separate from replaying the locked forecasts.
