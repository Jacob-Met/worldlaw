# A paired nominal command test in the Bilbao flume

The question is whether a model fitted to smaller and larger nominal commands can predict the finite change in first-arrival wave height when the experimenter switches from the uncorrected to the precompensated command. The two heights at each design are distinct physical recordings. The command values come from the released Target_Waves.xlsx setpoints. No calibrated paddle-displacement history was released. The endpoint is the authors' fitted pre-reflection wave height; the effect belongs to the complete command-to-gauge system.

The metadata-only join fixes 20 named design rows and 40 original recordings. The authors' own nonrecursive validation roster contains 23 direct pairs. Three lack a published commanded height and cannot enter a dose-response calculation. Retain their two measured wave heights as descriptive unbound pairs after the response opening. Two nested pairs under nose/ have identical design labels to direct files, no documented replacement/replicate rule and are outside the authors' discovery function. Preserve their paths and CRCs; do not select among them. Keep all twenty joined designs at all three prescribed depths. Never replace a missing or nonpositive endpoint. A missing matched endpoint or failed prediction makes the registered result incomplete.

A separate direction-identification idea was declined before calibration. The validation endpoint uses the generated section before the estimated first return. Development acquired eight channels on one timestamp vector, including an auxiliary probe at 16.3 m; the seven-station attenuation analysis omits that probe. P1/P2 label repeats and have separate clocks. The published reflection pipeline already uses generalized least squares, Goda–Suzuki and Mansard–Funke. Its phase information and response columns are unavailable to a command-only model.

## Models and fitting

The calibration domain is the 390 physical development records, grouped by the 199 unique depth, period, nominal-height conditions. Use the authors' precomputed generated-section five-period heights at seven stations (8,10,12,14,16,18,20 m). Average finite positive values within a condition and gauge, with equal condition weight. Retain counts of missing/nonpositive values by condition, gauge, and repeat. Period and depth conversions follow source filenames. The validation predictors receive only their nominal command, depth, period and station. All validation periods occur at the two recorded bracketing calibration depths.

The primary model is the composition of a monotone saturating entry relation and linear-plus-quadratic spatial attenuation. Write u=U/0.1 m and s=(x−8 m)/12 m. With F(k,s)=(1−exp(−ks))/k and F(0,s)=s, the model is H(U,x)=U*a*exp(−ks)/(1+u*(b+c*F(k,s))). Fit a,k,b,c separately on each required calibration (depth,period) cell by relative squared error on observed heights, using all amplitudes and seven stations jointly. Fixed start (0.95,0.05,0.02,0.02), bounds (1e−8,0,0,0) to (+infinity,20,100,100), SciPy trust-region reflective least squares with tolerances 1e−11 and max2000 evaluations. Require success, at least three distinct commands and three stations. Predict at the exact corresponding depth or linearly interpolate predicted heights in depth. These are effective coefficients from x=8 m onward. The boundary term includes the generator and preceding reach. No fitted coefficient is a measured friction, loss or paddle parameter.

Mandatory comparisons are the exact published compensation formula, the authentic end-to-end A, B and C formulas from results_equation.m, a no-attenuation homogeneous control, and two same-information training-profile interpolations. A and B are degree-one homogeneous: the released B friction coefficient varies as 1/H0, so its nominally nonlinear denominator cancels command scale. C is genuinely dose-dependent. Use the source's end-to-end alpha_gen_fun coefficients for A/B/C, visibly separate from calculate_H_awasys.m's coefficients. Fit global CB and beta_q on positive ki_exp finite calibration conditions using the published bounded relative-SSE objectives at six downstream stations, starting from observed H1 during calibration only. Expose solver tolerances and actual support. Do not use the authors' per-case parameters fitted to the held downstream profiles. Each empirical arm interpolates calibrated heights at the exact period and station within a depth, then linearly across bracketing depths. Use (a) piecewise linear height vs nominal amplitude, (b) shape-preserving PCHIP in log dose/log height. Both include all retained finite positive training levels. Never extrapolate outside amplitude/depth support. If a required arm is unsupported, record failure for that design.

## Decision and custody

For each of the 20 joined designs and each arm, lock two absolute predicted heights, their log ratio, and source, units, roster and per-array hashes before accessing any validation response column. The primary measured target is log(H_cor/H_unc). The primary gate is smaller equal-design mean absolute log-ratio error than every comparison; second gate is smaller maximum absolute paired error than every comparison. Third gate is smaller mean absolute log-height error across the 40 external records than every comparison, so a good ratio cannot hide a common amplitude bias. Strict comparisons, all 20 designs compulsory. Report absolute levels, per-depth and worst-pair results, percentage gain and every paired residual even on a negative. Separately report departures from the homogeneity contrast log(H_cor/H_unc)−log(U_cor/U_unc). With one released direct pair per design this is a finite experimental measurement, not a noise-free structural rejection or population estimate.

The published Validation_Results.xlsx columns H_unc/H_cor furnish the original author-defined outcome. Before the lock, restrict reads of that workbook to its header and case label. Use a separately implemented receiver for all 40 endpoint values and all model contrasts, with unchanged truth/reference source identities. No fitting, data correction or retuning on any corrected or uncorrected validation response.
