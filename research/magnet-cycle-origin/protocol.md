# MagNet 2023 cycle origin

A measured cycle has 1,024 flux-density samples in tesla, a frequency in hertz, a temperature in °C and a material identity. The final challenge predicts one positive volumetric loss in W/m³. Its organizer acquisition scripts align jointly measured voltage and current to the first maximum measured voltage before calculating flux and loss. Step 4 integrates voltage to obtain B and centers each B vector. Its scalar loss is the cycle mean of the paired product of voltage and current divided by effective volume. Simultaneously rotating the paired samples preserves that mean. The preliminary handbook specifies zero dc bias during acquisition; the supplied final dataset still has no independent bias channel. Its early 128-point, W/cm³ interface differs from the actual final 1,024-point files scored here in W/m³. The rotated test below reuses a cycle's measured loss and tests a changed coordinate origin. It supplies no second physical measurement, and the test inputs supply neither original current nor an independently varied DC-bias field.

The Paderborn HARDCORE submission won the official overall performance competition, which included model size. Twenty-three complete historical prediction sets have immutable entries in historical_sources.json. The Sydney physics-inspired MMINN earned the separate innovation award. The historical comparison reports numerical power-loss errors under the organizer evaluator. The original composite competition ranking uses additional criteria.

## Prospectively registered comparison

For the zero-based organizer row i and ASCII material letter M, the offset is one plus the first eight hexadecimal digits of SHA256("MagNet2023-origin-v1|M|i") interpreted in base 16 modulo 1,023. The shifted view rotates the stored B cycle right by that offset. The proposed canonical view starts at the first maximum of the circular centered difference of B, resolving exact ties by lexicographically greatest complete cyclic floating-point word. The wrong anchor applies the same tie rule to the maximum of B. Canonical views from unshifted and shifted arrays must match byte for byte. The centered difference is a voltage proxy. The even-length trapezoidal integration and absence of original voltage preclude identifying the actual voltage maximum uniquely from stored B in general.

The original submission runs a whole material batch, with its own batch-derived scalar and derivative denominators and one native TorchScript forward. Its waveform classification includes absolute sample positions. The March 10 maintained Paderborn implementation uses the same five training-only submission weight blobs and fixed per-material normalizers. Its comments describe extrema over the entire dataset; their fitting provenance is unresolved. The later March 17 models trained with final-test labels are excluded. Both Paderborn implementations and Sydney use all available frequency and temperature information. Sydney uses 128 downsampled flux values and 16 additional initialization points. Its precommitted CPU runtime uses the same prepared data tensors and batch size as the submission, with nonpinned host transport. Original and rotated are scored for all three model entry points. The two canonical arms use only the submitted Paderborn entry, giving eight forecasts per material.

The robustness criterion requires canonical dB to beat both the shifted and max-field controls at four or more of five material P95s and at the arithmetic mean of the five P95s. Independently, ordinary-coordinate compatibility requires the mean P95 to be at most the submitted original and every material P95 increment to be at most 0.5 percentage points. The joint gate requires both. Saved original comparison requires at most 0.02 percentage points of P95 deviation and maximum relative forecast difference at most 0.2% per material. The public scorer computes percentage errors against measured losses and reports unweighted mean, RMS, linear-interpolated 95th percentile and maximum. Aggregate materials receive equal weight. All original rows and worst coordinate tuples are retained.

A fixed descriptor challenge groups exact equal material/frequency/temperature/sorted B values/sorted absolute circular increments. For positive measurements m ≤ M in any one identical group, a single descriptor-only prediction has worst relative error at least (M−m)/(M+m), attained for the two extremes by their harmonic mean. This bound applies only to that descriptor and group's maximum error. Different order-sensitive flux traces, unsupplied quantities, and measurement variability can remain. An empty group search cannot supply a positive bound.
