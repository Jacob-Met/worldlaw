# Can one optical calibration predict the higher harmonics of a loudspeaker sweep?

A calibration learned from the fundamental predicted the relation between the archived displacement and velocity channels at the second and third harmonics of one LAUM loudspeaker sweep. A frequency-local complex calibration gave lower error than a two-pole, one-zero filter on both harmonics. Both held orders had measurable signal in the three bands from 80 to 640 Hz. The two higher bands failed the preregistered signal requirement. A single device, sweep and sensor supplied all three orders. [Author measurement and source](https://github.com/antonin-novak/LAUM_Loudspeaker_Parameters_Measurement/tree/13fbaaf1d0b7d065565b0b15e11ca912898cf298)

Each cell averages the relative complex RMS error of the supported bands with equal band weight. Each band error is the norm of the difference between predicted and measured velocity spectra divided by the norm of the measured spectrum. The fundamental has five eligible bands. Orders two and three have three each. The frozen success rule required the proposed filter to beat all four alternatives by 5% on *each* transfer order and stay within 10% of the strongest alternative in every eligible band. Its ratios to the best alternative were 1.50305 and 1.13158, respectively. Among supported cells the largest ratio, 2.04609, occurred at order two in the 160–320 Hz band. At its largest absolute-error bin, 234.375 Hz, the recorded velocity coefficient was 0.00007555−0.00390438i and the prediction was −0.00029448−0.00379592i, in the stored channel convention. The model and all five fixed bands remain available in the score table in `observed.json`.

## What the archive measures

The author’s acquisition code interleaves loudspeaker terminal voltage, terminal current, velocity and displacement on a four-channel DT9837 input scan at 48 kHz. Playback starts in a separate API call. The code specifies a five-second 10 Hz–20 kHz exponential sine phase law and a preceding 0.1 seconds of zeros. Its inverse sweep filter zeros the DC coefficient. The sample file holds four deconvolved impulse-response arrays of 244,800 little-endian doubles. Its descriptive metadata identifies the speaker as “Test.” It supplies no per-run calibration sheet, sensor serial number or acquisition trigger timestamp. Both the original 2023 commit and the 2024 code use the same sample blob and sweep specification. The author describes the displacement sensor as the source of membrane velocity. Absolute static position, independent optical ground truth and a common optical delay against the electrical channels remain unresolved. All comparisons use the original channel scaling and a fitted relative gain. [Original release](https://github.com/antonin-novak/LAUM_Loudspeaker_Parameters_Measurement/tree/08c17c61a6d077a151a46314a32735d4e2c63420)

Before opening numerical signals, we fixed the clock, window, noise gates, models and score. On the voltage channel alone, the largest absolute sample within the author’s first second occurred at sample 3,530 (73.54 ms). It was also the global absolute maximum. The packet RMS of 0.01461 exceeded the larger background RMS, 0.00003023, by a factor of 483. For order n the center moves earlier by L ln(n), where L = 5/ln(2000) seconds, with circular indexing. The rounded centers for orders two and three were 226,444 and 213,641. An optical spectrum uses output frequency f and its corresponding drive frequency is f/n. Relative optical transfer uses the same segment for both channels, so the common sub-sample choice of time origin cancels from an ideal ratio. Finite packet truncation can still affect the result. [Author sweep derivation](https://www.ant-novak.com/pages/sss/)

Each segment began 576 samples before its frozen center and contained 4,096 samples under a Tukey window with taper fraction 0.20. Each motion channel needed at least four times its own band energy from *each* of two identically windowed background segments 95 and 175 ms later. Both higher-order packets passed at 80–160, 160–320 and 320–640 Hz. At 1,280–2,560 Hz the order-two power ratios to the larger background were 0.870 for displacement and 0.881 for velocity. At order three they were 3.185 and 0.901. The measurement-dependent support rule was frozen before opening signals. All 180 energy measurements, all 75 band errors including the unsupported bands, and both off-packet controls remain in the replay. Contamination by leakage or overlapping response tails remains possible.

For a tapered displacement x and continuous-time derivative, integration by parts gives the velocity predictor D(f) = i2πf FFT(w x) − FFT(w′ x), evaluated here on a uniform sampling grid. A non-bin-centered sinusoidal control had relative error 1.54×10⁻⁷ with this correction and 0.01358 when omitting the window derivative. Each fitted parametric filter has a real signed gain and every fit uses the fundamental alone. The fourth model adds a zero and a second pole to the delayed low-pass response. Its fitted zero was close to the 20 kHz search ceiling, with poles near 1.22 kHz. Their individual values do not establish physical modes. The local comparator estimates a complex gain by weighted least squares over neighboring fundamental bins, with fixed Gaussian width one-sixth octave. The independent reader uses direct complex exponentials in place of the FFT, reconstructs all 3,180 complex predictions, 180 powers and 75 scores, and verifies every worst-bin index.

## What the original electrical fit identifies

We separately executed both released author estimators on the first 48,000 samples of the electrical and velocity impulse responses. The 2023 Leach/mass–spring and 2024 Leach/fractional versions use the original 650 µs velocity advance and their original force-factor grid. Both returned 4.75 T·m and a 67 Hz measured resonance under the source calibration convention. Their fitted electrical-impedance relative RMS errors over 9,980 bins from 20 to 9,999 Hz were 0.08042 and 0.08039 respectively. Both had their largest absolute error at 9,973 Hz, about 2.40 Ω. The optical comparison above evaluates a different observable. These electrical figures describe fits to the same data used to set the parameters.

With force factor B, mechanical impedance Zm and electrical impedance Ze, the observed transfer ratios are V/I = B/Zm and U/I = Ze + B²/Zm. For any 0 < c < 1, set B′ = cB, Zm′ = cZm, and Ze′ = Ze + (1−c)B²/Zm. Both terminal transfer ratios are exactly preserved. If the original impedances are positive real and the mechanical admittance is well-defined, this also preserves passivity. For a concrete example use B = 3, c = 2/5, Ze = 4 + s/100 and Zm = 2 + s/10 + 200/s. The additional electrical branch is a passive parallel RLC with R = 27/10, L = 27/1000 and C = 1/54 in consistent units. The alternative has B′ = 6/5. Thus even the complete single-load spectrum leaves force factor ambiguous in this broader passive class. The author’s restricted electrical and mechanical parameterizations provide extra information. The measured relative optical transport does not distinguish among the equivalent electromechanical models.

## Replay

Use Git, Python 3.11 and the package versions below. The script fetches two immutable author commits, authenticates the source data, reconstructs all three packets and both author fits, and compares all 75 new scores and worst-bin frequencies to the recorded results. Numeric data come from the publisher repository. The replay reads the four hashed array buffers without executing pickle reducers. Full complex forecasts and intermediate reports are written to the chosen output directory.

```sh
uv run --python 3.11 --no-project --with numpy==1.26.4 --with scipy==1.13.1 --with matplotlib==3.9.4 python research/laum-optical-harmonic-transfer/replay.py /tmp/laum-replay
```
