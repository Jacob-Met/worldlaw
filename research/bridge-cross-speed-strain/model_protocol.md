# Prospective comparison specification

**Endpoint.** Predict the change in strain at the five previously fixed gauges, given the actual truck load state and the whole measured optical traversal. Evaluate all ten loaded fast passages individually and both nominal speeds separately. Both testing speeds exceed every development speed. No observed test strain, accelerometer waveform or held-response alignment enters a forecast.

**Registration.** The 10 Hz front range establishes an affine position–time curve by robust fitting of decreasing ranges. The immutable implementation uses its physical range window, nominal-speed constraints, minimum travel and residual rule for every case. The entry and exit barriers supply independent time checks. At high speed the rear tandem need not generate distinct laser samples. Preserve obstacle-support residuals, station fit scatter, partial optical coverage and the 0.1 s timing resolution. The resulting clock permits an offline conditional prediction. A claim about on-vehicle real-time forecasting would require a separate stream-causal registration.

**Scoring interval.** Use the intersection of the observed optical range with the interval from 2 s before the first axle enters through 2 s after the last axle exits. Each waveform is expressed relative to its own first observed 0.5 s in that interval. Compute this baseline on forecasts and observations separately. The target is a within-record change even for an already loaded bridge at the start of the record. Keep every actual observation time, record missingness by gauge, and give any unsupported case an explicit outcome. A registered held case without a qualified clock or observations prevents a complete positive verdict.

**Candidate.** Jointly fit the two measured axle-load patterns. Translate a 2 m cubic spatial basis by the three axle positions and sum with the statically weighed masses. Use both its instantaneous value and two deviations from it after causal unit-DC, second-order oscillator filtering at 3.003480 and 3.845675 Hz. Those fixed medians come from the independently released January–April modal tracking period, before the April 25 truck tests. Fix the corresponding damping ratios to the masked historical medians, 2.06537% and 4.43589% respectively. Figure 13 explicitly plots percent. The source series spans January 1 through April 21 and includes 5,007 and 5,250 valid observations per mode. Use an explicitly zero prehistory ahead of the spatial basis. Each gauge has its own fitted coefficients. The independently measured frequencies constrain temporal behavior. The fitted spatial coefficients, static axle weights and historical damping do not identify actual dynamic tyre force or a unique vibrational mode.

**Compulsory controls.** Predict zero strain change. Warp the authors' mean crawl station profiles by measured speed, and do the same for the 5.6 m/s profiles. Fit a separate smooth spatial curve per load state. Fit the static axle-weighted influence-line model, the same model with velocity and squared-velocity terms, and an unrestricted regularized finite-impulse-response model on the identical axle-weighted spatial basis. The finite-impulse-response model includes 14 delays through 1.5 s. Finally, fit the same two oscillator poles to independent curves for each load, which tests the contribution of weighed-axle sharing. All controls get the same optical coordinates, grouping and training data.

**Fitting and gates.** Train by equal-passage ridge regression. Use a fixed 50 Hz time-bin representative for the fit and reconstruct the forecasts at 200 Hz. Report metrics at every available target observation, including the worst signed error for each arm, speed and gauge. To select ridge regularization, reserve the last numbered development run in each of the four weight/speed cells, fit the remainder, and minimize the unweighted mean of passage-by-gauge RMSE over all four validation runs. Use the fixed grid 0.0001, 0.001, 0.01, 0.1 and 1.0 for each arm, with the smaller value resolving exact ties. Refit the chosen arm parameters on all 31 development runs; every case and each gauge receives its fixed weight even when acquisition durations differ. Retain all validation attempts. The candidate must beat every comparator's equal-passage, equal-gauge mean RMSE over the ten held runs, as well as the strongest comparator on each held speed separately and its worst-run mean over five gauges. All comparisons are strict; an exact tie fails. The mean/RMS convention reports native µm/m. A missing held result fails completeness. The target is waveform transfer on the measured runs. No independent population or structural-safety conclusion is registered.

**Optical qualification.** All 41 runs yielded a supported trajectory before any strain values were opened. On the two held groups, observed actual speeds were 10.621–10.780 and 13.135–13.562 m/s. The worst reflector fit RMS was 0.345 m across those ten acquisitions. The largest absolute difference from any nearest observed short-range barrier return at the expected axle time was 0.147 s, with the finite 10 Hz laser cadence and merged tyre returns retained. The partial third unloaded crawl had 1.076 s pre-entry optical coverage. All five later unloaded medium-speed records started 0.125–0.699 s after estimated front-axle entry. The per-record change endpoint preserves all of them. The largest optical fit RMS over the whole source, 0.751 m, occurred on the first unloaded crawl.
