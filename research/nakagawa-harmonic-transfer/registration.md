## Continuous response and finite measurements

With a fixed quasi-static map of instantaneous squared heater current, sinusoidal drive, stable reference and common linear readout of the voltage harmonics, let A be proportional to squared RMS heater command. Let U and Y be the second and fourth coefficients in the paper's RMS sign convention. For a response monomial of degree m in squared current, expanding the cosine powers gives Y/U = −(m−1)/(m+2). All nonconstant second coefficients are nonzero. For polynomial responses and hence continuous responses on a compact amplitude interval by uniform approximation,

    Y(A) = −U(A) + 3 A⁻² ∫₀ᴬ t U(t) dt.

The normalization factor in A cancels for each curve. An unobserved lower integration segment produces one A⁻² term. Constant readout offsets produce an additional constant. Subtracting any affine function from U therefore changes the Volterra feature only by those two nuisance terms. Exact recovery of unspecified behavior between sampled amplitudes is impossible. For any finite positive nodes A_j, choose U(A)=A∏(A−A_j)². It vanishes on the measured nodes, is generated by a polynomial static law, and gives strictly positive Y at each node. The exact rational witness in theorem.py checks four nodes and the coefficient identity through degree 48. This result concerns a family of synthetic static maps. It supplies no lower bound on physical errors.

Experimental integration uses a nine-point local quadratic fit, then the exact integral of t times the piecewise-linear interpolant. Smoothing uses only U from the same sweep. This introduces finite-mesh and smoothing errors. It does not integrate the unknown continuum spectrum exactly. The primary fits a constant, A⁻² term and free gain on the low-drive Y values. This absorbs fixed relative instrument scale/sign and allows either gain sign; the primary tests relative shape under the calibrated comparison. A passing result would not authenticate electrical phase, identify a unique intrinsic thermoelectric coefficient or establish vanishing thermal memory. Frequency-dependent transfer, quadrature projection, drive errors, temperature history, noise in U and subtractive conditioning can all affect the result. A single fixed gain cannot absorb an amplitude-dependent phase. Keep the fixed +1/−1 gain arms and the unsmoothed integral.
