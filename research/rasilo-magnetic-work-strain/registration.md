## What the field determines

Let λ be the measured longitudinal magnetostriction, σ the longitudinal stress in Pa, and H and B the corresponding magnetic field and flux density in A/m and T. Set a=(1+ν)/E, E=183 GPa and ν=0.34. Script8 uses v=aσ+3λ/2, τ=−λ/a and dψ=H dB+τ dv. Adding the exact differential of 3λ²/(4a) therefore gives a potential with differential H dB−λ dσ. For two stresses, the negative difference of their two magnetic works integrated from B₀ to B equals the stress integral of λ(B,σ)−λ(B₀,σ). The identity assumes a single reversible material potential for the processed surface under matched thermodynamic conditions.

With only discrete stress boundaries, exact relative strain at an individual stress remains undetermined. Add f(B)p(σ) to that potential. For any chosen held stress take p to have a simple root there and double roots at every other measured stress. No measured H boundary value changes. Its σ derivative changes at the chosen held stress alone. The perturbation changes relative strain when f(B) differs from f(B₀). This is a smooth-potential nonidentification witness. It gives no physical perturbation magnitude or unrestricted stability guarantee. geometry.py verifies 221 exact rational derivative identities. The predicted interval-average quantity would require continuous stress observations for direct verification. The scored endpoint here is pointwise measured relative strain under an explicit trapezoidal stress-quadrature closure.

## Frozen procedures

The primary integrates H over the uneven B axis by trapezoids. At every B, the 16 stress intervals yield estimated mean relative strains. The trapezoidal stress closure requires each adjacent pair of predicted nodal relative strains to average to that interval mean. Its one-dimensional null space alternates signs between neighbors; least squares to the four calibrating strain curves fixes the single remaining coefficient. No labeled curve selects the procedure.

All eight comparators receive the same information. Two differentiate either a natural cubic or PCHIP interpolant of the accumulated magnetic work with respect to stress. Three interpolate the anchor strain using linear, PCHIP or natural cubic interpolation and preserve the prescribed extrapolation at 90 and 100 MPa. Another interpolates anchor residuals around the natural-cubic magnetic derivative. A fixed ridge estimate balances the squared errors of the magnetic intervals and the squared deviations from linear anchor interpolation with unit weights in microstrain. The ninth arm reconstructs the author’s joint energy-derivative spline family. It fits only the four exposed strain-bearing stresses. It uses the authors' scaling and tensor-product derivative collocation, cubic optimal-recovery knots, PCHIP transformation onto regular v and joint least squares of both measured derivative components. The implementation solves the stacked system directly; the published MATLAB example uses normal equations. At a held stress the published plot uses measured λ to provide v, which would supply its own answer here. The executable comparison instead solves the resulting quadratic implicit coordinate equation. Multiple real roots are resolved by proximity to anchor-only PCHIP; that same interpolation is the declared fallback when no real root exists. Save rank, multiple-root/fallback counts and extrapolative roots. No claim of identical full-data MATLAB outputs or measured dynamics.
