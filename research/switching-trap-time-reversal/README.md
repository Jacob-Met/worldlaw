## Observable and conditional forecasts

For a centered stationary scalar coordinate, define A(t) = E[X(0)³X(t) − X(0)X(t)³], in nm⁴. Reversal changes its sign. Every stationary scalar Gaussian process has A(t)=0. A nonzero value can witness reversal asymmetry, while zero at the chosen lags leaves other time-asymmetric statistics possible. This observable supplies no direct entropy-production estimate.

Write X=dU+G, with a fixed linear response U to a unit active drive, half-separation d, and independent centered stationary Gaussian G. The mixed terms in the two fourth moments match using the equal-time variances and scalar lagged covariances. Thus A_X(t)=d⁴A_U(t). Adding a constant μ to a stationary Z changes its raw A by 3μ E[Z(0)²Z(t)−Z(0)Z(t)²]. Centering matters for asymmetric forcing. Dependence between the active and Gaussian contributions, nonlinear observation and amplitude-dependent filtering require additional information.

The same d⁴ contrast holds in expectation for an equal-length, equal-grid, sample-centered lag sum under these assumptions. Subtracting the record mean commutes with time reversal. The centered Gaussian covariance and the active signal’s raw second moments remain invariant under reversal of both indices. Every mixed even-even term in the expanded fourth polynomial depends only on those second moments. The lag sum pairs it with its reversed term. Gaussian odd moments vanish by centering and the pure Gaussian term is reversal invariant. This removes the Gaussian component from that expected comparison. A finite-record statistic can still differ from the population generator curve.

The supplemental Table I uses mobility 4×10⁴ nm/(pN s), stiffness 0.056 pN/nm, and total switching rate w=20/s. With reported symmetric switching, each one-direction rate is 10/s. The ideal actively displaced coordinate obeys dx/dt=a(ds−x), s=±1, a=2240/s. The generator closes on x, s, x³ and sx². The code uses stationary mixed moments and a 55-digit matrix exponential. At 2 ms its ideal predictions are −36.228, −8,285.924 and −2,121,196.502 nm⁴ at the three respective separations. The full eight-lag prediction vector is supplied. Thermal Gaussian motion cancels under the stated assumptions. These numbers refer to the latent, unfiltered population.

## Why the measurement chain matters

The authors’ sign convention is F=k(λ−x), so the released lab-frame bead position combines the measured trap center and force as x=λ−F/k. With separate linear channel responses Hλ and HF, the reconstructed position is HF x+(Hλ−HF)λ. Both optical signals were acquired on a board reported at 100 kHz bandwidth. That value does not identify either phase response or the relative group delay. A common linear filter can preserve between-amplitude d⁴ scaling while changing the lag curve.

A finite exact counterexample uses a reversible two-state chain with adjacent correlation 1/2. The causal two-tap filters (3/4,1/4) and (1/4,3/4) have unit DC gain and identical squared frequency magnitude 5/8+(3/8)cos(ω). They give opposite observed one-sample contrasts, −9/64 and +9/64 in normalized units. This shows that spectral magnitude and bandwidth alone cannot fix the sign of this fourth moment. It does not establish that either filter was used in the apparatus.

## Prospective comparison and admission

The premeasurement contrast uses the eight lags 0.25, 0.5, 1, 2, 4, 8, 16 and 32 ms. The five 70 nm traces would supply an equally weighted, sample-centered empirical donor at each lag. Its predictions multiply the donor curve by (18/70)⁴ or (280/70)⁴ for the ten and five held traces respectively. A reversible zero curve is the other required comparator. For each held trace, average absolute errors over all eight lags after dividing by its own nominal half-separation to the fourth power. Give each of the two held amplitudes equal weight. The ideal curve must strictly improve the equal-amplitude mean and the worst amplitude relative to both empirical and zero controls. Preserve every trace, eight-component residual, raw nm⁴ curve and any missing-data refusal. This donor is a defined rule; no empirical donor curve was available to estimate.
