# Which vortex-impulse terms does a planar velocity contour determine?

For an oriented annulus D in a fixed laboratory plane, let t follow the positively oriented boundary (clockwise on the inner circle), n be its outward normal, and ω=∂x v−∂y u. Put Mx=∫D xω dA and My=∫D yω dA. Green's theorem gives ∫xω=∮x u_t ds−∫v dA and ∫yω=∮y u_t ds+∫u dA. If u_x+v_y=0, apply the divergence theorem to y(u,v) and x(u,v). Both first moments then have exact contour expressions:

Mx = ∮(x u_t−y u_n) ds,   My = ∮(y u_t+x u_n) ds.

These identities apply to the total signed moments over the annulus. They do not recover the vorticity distribution or an individual vortex's share. Rigid rotation or translation enters via the actual no-slip velocity on the inner contour. With two-dimensional divergence δ=u_x+v_y, add ∫yδ to the first contour expression, and subtract ∫xδ from the second. A planar measurement of a three-dimensional incompressible flow can have δ=−∂z w at midspan. An explicit smooth counterexample with unchanged contour trace is u=0, v=f(x,y), w=−z ∂y f for compactly supported smooth f inside the missing annulus. The three-dimensional field is divergence-free, w=0 on the midplane, and Mx=−∫f can vary, so even zero out-of-plane velocity on the measured plane would not establish the planar identity there.

In a fixed complete physical frame this is a moment-transformation identity. The force-balance observable requires time derivatives, displaced-body inertia, spanwise integration, and sufficiently closed outer flow or finite-control-volume boundary fluxes. A moving origin adds a term proportional to circulation and translation. A source-independent fixed time/distance alignment, coordinate axes, inertial subtraction, valid annular contour with complete velocity and uncertainty from spatial and temporal sampling are eligibility requirements. The authors already use near-wall boundary velocities and a competing interpolation-based impulse estimate. Noca, Shiels and Jeon give established finite-volume/finite-surface force formulations from velocity. This study does not claim discovery of the force or moment transform.
