# Conditional wave-radiation hypothesis

The model is a 0.300 m diameter sphere whose center is at the still free surface at equilibrium. Let z be the upward center displacement in metres from that position and R = 0.150 m. At a fixed still plane the geometric submerged cap height is h = min(2R,max(0,R−z)). Integrating its circular cross sections gives V(h) = πh²(R−h/3) m³. The still-plane waterplane area inside the partial-immersion interval is A = π(R²−z²) m² and vanishes when fully dry or fully submerged. The positive displaced-volume source, Q = dV/dt = −A dz/dt, is in m³/s. It is positive during immersion. For a discrete causal interval, use [V(z_now)−V(z_previous)]/Δt. At release use the rest condition and zero first increment; the initial submerged volume does not itself generate an impulse.

At equilibrium V0 = 2πR³/3. On the interior V−V0 = −πR²z + πz³/3. Thus a two-input empirical convolution of increments of z and z³ strictly contains the proposed geometric convolution on that interval when given the same temporal basis. Globally let s = clip(z,−R,R). Then V−V0 = −πR²s + πs³/3 at every height, including dry and fully submerged limits. An empirical convolution of the two increments of s and s³ contains the capped geometric driver exactly, with identical impulse and clipping conventions. A size-limited training course can test whether tying these two transfer kernels to their geometric coefficient improves prediction. It cannot establish that the gauges measure the still-plane flux, identify a free-space radiation operator or isolate a unique source of error. The actual moving surface, free-surface effects, pressure and velocity field, boundary reflections, air entrainment and optical sensor calibration need not follow the approximation.

The primary conditional role is an offline, motion-conditioned prediction, with causal access to the measured high-release motion through each prediction instant only. The same-input empirical controls receive exactly that motion. A distinct release-only class uses archived nominal-release RANS2, RANS3, RANS4 and RANS5 waveforms. A source-trained transfer can also use each model's own predicted heave and be paired against its original free-surface forecast. These numbers answer different prediction questions. The archived, full future body-motion signal is never described as supplied at release.

The paper's Figure 8 and its source PDF place the gauges at three successive 0.6 m offsets, with WG3 nearest and WG1 farthest from the sphere, in a finite 13.00 by 8.44 m basin at 0.900 m depth.
