# Apparatus and comparison units

Kramer and colleagues released the experiment through IEA Ocean Energy Systems. A 300 mm diameter ballasted aluminum sphere floated half submerged at equilibrium. The 13.00 m by 8.44 m laboratory basin contained 0.900 m of water. The wavemaker was inactive. Three fixed gauges, numbered from farthest (WG1) to nearest (WG3), recorded surface elevation alongside optical body-motion tracking. Figure 8 shows 0.600 m spacing along the gauge line. A fixed-height release from rest excites both the body and water: the three gauge records are simultaneous responses to the same trial. Four repetitions at one height do not constitute four independent release heights or four different instruments. [Experiment and drawings](https://doi.org/10.3390/en14020269)

The experiment files label t in seconds and x3, WG1, WG2 and WG3 in metres. The released tabular grid is 500 Hz. The paper describes four optical cameras running at 300 frames/s, so the 500 Hz file grid does not establish five hundred independent optical measurements per second. Recorded t=0 was used without a fitted timing translation. There was no smoothing or normalization by held gauge height. Restricting the early diagnostic to t≤4.56 s uses the authors' nominal period and reflection discussion. It does not isolate all direct or reflected wave components. [Institutional benchmark archive](https://www.oceanenergysystems.org/documents/55575-datafile.zip/)

There are twelve measured raw recordings on three nominal release heights. Eleven blind modeling submissions supply a total of 31 numerical files. Seven high-release submissions give just a time axis and vertical body displacement. Four also supply the three free-surface gauge predictions. RANS3 participated at the high height only. The four gauge entrants are all retained, with their own native coverage. The independent producer consumes no fitted competition model, and uses the submitted predictions directly where indicated.
