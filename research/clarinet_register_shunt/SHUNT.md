# What an isolated linear register-hole shunt predicts

At a single frequency write the downstream acoustic admittance as y. Replacing the closed register hole with an open one changes local admittance by a complex scalar d. The upstream bore and the impedance adaptor transform the junction admittance to the measured complex normalized impedance by a fractional linear map T. If the upstream transfer T and downstream section are the same in both acquisitions, the measured transition is M=T∘(y↦y+d)∘T⁻¹. This uses finite nonzero determinant for T and linear, time-invariant, small-signal impedance. A closed chimney can have nonzero admittance because d is the difference of the two side branches.

For a matrix representative M=(a,b;c,d₀), repeated fixed point requires (a−d₀)²+4bc=0. A nonidentity such map has exactly one fixed point r=T(∞). In the coordinate 1/(z−r) the hole increment is a translation, including the r=∞ case by using z directly. Two distinct outlet pairs alone generically leave two possible fixed points. A third pair can resolve that algebraic ambiguity if the data carry independent information. Equal input impedances, an imperceptible hole perturbation and nearly dependent calibration loads can make an accurate prediction impossible even when this parameter count is adequate.

If three hole diameters share exactly the same upstream reference transfer T, their three maps have the same r and commute as fractional linear transformations. The experiment used three manufactured upper bodies and a common nominal register position. Equal nominal dimensions do not establish identical T. A per-body shunt map and a freely fitted general two-port map are therefore separate controls. A change in series arm or longitudinal wave path, radiation involving the outlet, excitation/amplitude dependence, or measurement calibration can break the repeated-point condition. Conversely, a fitted repeated point cannot uniquely locate a hole or identify a nonlinear loss.

A magnitude-only archive cannot in general support this phase-sensitive transfer without stronger source restrictions. For a normalized unit shunt, M(z)=z/(1+z). The strictly passive impedances z=1 and z=exp(iπ/3) both have magnitude one. The predicted output magnitudes are respectively 1/2 and 1/√3. Even this exact known shunt does not give the observed output magnitude from a single input magnitude in general. This pointwise ambiguity does not address inference from complete broadband phase-recoverability assumptions.
