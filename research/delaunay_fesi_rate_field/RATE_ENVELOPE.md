# Rate-dependent magnetic field on one reversal branch

The authors recorded primary current across a noninductive shunt and secondary induced voltage. Their cover identifies four exported vectors, time in seconds, H in A/m, B in T and dB/dt in T/s. Each of the two 3%-Si rings comprises four laminations. The measured lamination thicknesses and resistivities differ. The low-frequency classical field coefficients d²/(12ρ) are 0.020518838136112813 and 0.005469444444444444 A·s/(m·T) for the 0.3471-mm and 0.1969-mm sheets. Using these two specimens alone cannot separate thickness, resistivity and grade effects.

## Conditional interval

Compare three acquisitions of the same ring at the same induction B on the same ascending or descending branch. Suppose the reversal history and initial magnetic state match, and the equilibrium response does not change with frequency, temperature, elapsed time or instrument transfer. Let s be the common sign of dB/dt and v its magnitude. For this calculation assume 0 < v1 < v2 < v3. Under uniform induction in a lamination the classical term in sH is cv, c=d²/(12ρ). Consider a statistical excess field of the form g(v)=a(√(1+uv)−1), with a,u≥0; the square-root asymptote and nonnegative mixtures are included by limits. The static field is a free additive constant at this branch state. Its cancellation requires matching the reversal history, not merely the current B.

For Y=sH−cv, let D=Y2−Y1. A necessary condition is D≥0. The next increment must lie between D(√v3−√v2)/(√v2−√v1) and D(v3−v2)/(v2−v1). To see this, rationalize the finite difference: its ratio is the linear increment ratio times (√(v1+h)+√(v2+h))/(√(v2+h)+√(v3+h)), h=1/u. The logarithmic derivative of each adjacent square-root sum is 1/(2√((vi+h)(vj+h))). Consequently the ratio is increasing with h, from the square-root limit to the linear limit. Every admissible nonnegative mixture has a ratio that is a positively weighted mean of its component ratios. A zero second-point increment predicts zero third-point excess increment in this class. A negative D contradicts the class already on the two source points.

The measured primary-field waveform supplies the target. Aggregate loop loss integrates away information about the phase and local signs of the field error. The interval above is a conditional response envelope, not a noise confidence interval. A mismatch in reversal points, integrator phase, apparatus delay or spatial magnetic penetration can violate the premises. The same nominal PWM fundamental does not establish matching nested reversals, particularly if the carrier ratio differs. No result for magnetic fields is asserted at registration.
