# Printed ice aggregates and the direction of fall

Two numerical methods attempted to predict how 20 printed aggregates orient during descent. Neither met its preregistered numerical requirements on all 20 source geometries. All 128 measured orientation records remain unscored. The surface-integral method gave reproducible directions on nine geometries. Its failures on the remaining eleven occurred before comparison with the experiment.

Three digital rules for filling intersecting surfaces agreed on bulk properties to within 6.956×10⁻⁸ in relative volume and 2.762×10⁻⁸ in absolute inertia ratio. Those small differences are contrary evidence for a large bulk effect. Converting them to an orientation error bound would require information about the manufactured surface and certified errors in the fluid tensor. The experiment's orientation records cannot resolve that input question prospectively.

## Source and experimental unit

The Nottingham deposit contains twenty named STL files and one worksheet covering 128 runs. The recovered archive is 792,963 bytes with SHA-256 `4c949ee53e6abfd7951fb14bb3d816937505f89ba05ccc604537c9c9847e0172`. The workbook provides calculated inertia ratios and observed Reynolds and Best numbers. It also contains per-run minimum, mean and maximum angles between vertical and the three principal inertia axes, plus a maximum-span angle. Only the shape names, inertia ratios and Reynolds-number domain entered this qualification. XML containers were parsed as a whole. The receiver selected no orientation cells. All runs on a given shape remain together. [Nottingham dataset](https://doi.org/10.17639/nott.7199)

The methods differ across the two original studies. Westbrook and Sephton dropped 22 PLA designs in a cylindrical 28 cm by 63 cm tank, estimated speed by transit time, and checked CAD dimensions against calipers. Their measured print density was 1,252 ± 3 kg/m³. A reported Reynolds/Best pair averages five drops. They assessed the preferred falling orientation visually to define the projected area. Changing the timed vertical interval tested settling distance and proximity to the base. Per-design physical scale and wall corrections still require the source tables. [2017 methods](https://doi.org/10.1002/2017GL074130)

McCorquodale and Westbrook studied 844 experiments on 118 geometries, usually 1–3 cm across, printed in photopolymer resin with a measured density of 1,174 ± 2 kg/m³. Three synchronized cameras observed a 20 cm cube about 1.5 m below the surface of a 0.4 × 0.4 × 1.8 m tank. The methods estimate a sidewall effect below 1% at the lowest investigated Reynolds number. The authors used fall-normal projected span and area reconstructed from the observed pose. For unsteady falls they used trajectory means of each measured quantity. They screened physical mass against digital-model volume times resin density. The rotating frame and principal moments were calculated from geometry. Per-print sliced masks and torsional inertia measurements are absent from the identified deposit. [TRAIL methods](https://doi.org/10.1002/qj.3935) [TRAIL drag study](https://doi.org/10.1002/qj.3936)

The three advertised 2017/2021 geometry/data ZIPs returned HTTP 403 in the original intake. The Nottingham authors explicitly link the twenty shapes to the wider TRAIL campaign. Stout et al. also describe reanalysing the camera campaign. Their paper reports 354 plate experiments, whereas our first-two-fields-only audit of the accompanying CSV recovered 1,068 distinct run IDs and 28 named shape families plus a blank shape value. An exact crosswalk to the 844 drag runs or the 22 older PLA designs was unavailable. The 2024 archive is unsuited as an assumed independent validation set. Only its header and run/shape identities were selected. Its tabled area ratio uses observations made during steady falls. [2024 study and data](https://doi.org/10.5194/acp-24-11133-2024)

## What an observed projection can identify

For a homogeneous no-slip ellipsoid in unbounded creeping flow, let the semiaxes be a, b, c and let qᵢ be the squared components of the **vertical** unit vector in the body frame. Centered buoyancy and inversion symmetry give zero angular velocity. The horizontal shadow obeys A²/(πabc)² = Σqᵢ/aᵢ². Under uniform gravity, the vertical mobility is Σqᵢmᵢ, even when translation also has a horizontal component. The Stokes result assumes zero inertia and unlimited fluid. It supplies an identifiability example independently of the experimental Reynolds-number range. [Ellipsoid mobility](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-fluid-mechanics/article/sedimentation-of-spheroids-in-newtonian-fluids-with-spatially-varying-viscosity/A6FDE4796BFC80C65B5D32383EE6ACD9)

Writing D(s) = √[(a²+s)(b²+s)(c²+s)], the scaled mobility 16πμmᵢ is the integral from zero to infinity of [1 + 1/(1+s/aᵢ²)]/D(s). The integrand is strictly convex in 1/aᵢ² for s>0. As all orientation weights in the simplex are attainable, the area–vertical-mobility pairs fill exactly the triangle with axis-aligned vertices. At fixed area the lower bound is the broken line through adjacent ordered vertices and the upper bound is the chord between the extremes.

For semiaxes (3,2,1), the middle-axis pose and squared direction (27/32,0,5/32) both give area 3π. Directed integral bounds for their scaled mobilities are [1.3730780,1.3739114] and [1.4425040,1.4434037]. At least one of the two absolute errors of any shared point prediction is at least 0.0342963. Independent Carlson symmetric integrals give 1.3734946242 and 1.4429537801.

For a **known** triaxial shape, area and the major width of the projected ellipse determine both projected eigenvalues. Their sum is Σaᵢ²−Σqᵢaᵢ² and their product is (abc)²Σqᵢ/aᵢ². Together with Σqᵢ=1, the two moments identify q when the three axes are distinct. Repeated body axes leave rotations within their subspace unresolved. An exact receiver reconstructs 153 rational test directions. Across **different shapes**, even the entire shadow and volume can coincide. A unit sphere and its vertical shear z→z+3x/2 have unit-disk shadows and volume 4π/3. The shear has semiaxes (2,1,1/2) and squared vertical direction (4/5,0,1/5). Its scaled mobility is 2.5491287681, against the sphere's exact 8/3.

For a general body centered on the applied buoyant weight, the torque-free translational resistance is the Schur complement Rtt−Rtr Rrr⁻¹ Rrt. A nonzero translation–rotation block can change orientation in time. A fixed-orientation mobility interval is then an instantaneous bound. In the reported aggregate cohort Re ranges from about 5.129 to 1,927.996, so we did not fit a creeping-flow settling law.

The volume-based Galileo number also illustrates why input provenance matters. For the common-operand definitions Ar=4A/(πD²) and Be=2gVΔρρfD²/(Aμ²), Gaᵥ²=3BeAr/4. The cancellation uses the same A and D. Products of separately averaged inverse and direct factors can differ. For equal weights on Ar=1/4 and 1, the inflation is 25/16. No reconstructed Galileo number was used in any forecast.

## Prescribed-flow hypothesis and the two numerical tests

With a fixed laboratory translation of speed U, body-frame vertical direction n, and unit-density added-mass tensor M, fluid kinetic energy is U² nᵀMn/2. Varying body orientation at prescribed translation gives torque U²(Mn)×n. With positive angular damping this closure favours the largest added-mass eigenspace. Inviscid free motion supplies no terminal speed or dissipative selection by itself. Wake torques, translation–rotation coupling, lateral motion and walls may change a freely falling body's preferred direction. The study registered this as a geometry-only forecast hypothesis.

Both methods predicted the unsigned mean inclination for every geometry. Within 1.5% of the largest added-mass eigenvalue they averaged inclinations over the leading subspace. For a pair of solid-inertia axes closer than 2% of the largest inertia, the primary comparison uses its isolated complementary axis. The original plan also retained all three raw axes, every run, equal-shape mean and worst-shape error. Baselines comprised vertical maximum solid inertia, the largest sampled genuine projected union area, and constant and kernel predictors trained outside each source-name family. The empirical gates were never reached.

The largest voxel drift was 16.2801° on Cr14. The coarse and fine tensors differed by 0.182502 in operator norm, compared with leading eigengaps 0.013118 and 0.115600. The selected subspace changed from dimension two to one. Cr77 and Cr92 drifted 14.4622° and 13.4572°. Fine occupied volumes were within 2% of their normalized source volumes in all three. Each refused potential solve had an isolated fluid voxel with zero diagonal. These pockets diagnose the numerical grid; their identity as physical cavities was unverified. Sq1's unquantized polygon union also refused.

The surface method integrates the triangle solid angle and single-layer edge logarithms analytically. It uses the true projected triangle union with prospectively fixed precision grids and the same angular search. On a 512-panel ellipsoid and sphere its relative tensor errors were 1.033% and 1.376%. A rotated synthetic tensor agreed to 4.43×10⁻¹¹ relative, doubling all lengths multiplied the tensor by eight, and overlapped front/back polygon fixtures had the exact expected covered area. The nine completed physical meshes passed the reciprocity and union-precision checks. Nine of the eleven surface refusals were near one complete 4π winding; the other two closure residuals were 4.07×10⁻⁵ and 3.54×10⁻⁴ radians.

## Why edge closure was insufficient

Each mesh has one edge-connected triangle complex after removal of exact zero-area triangles. Cr44 had 140 such triangles. Independent rational rays show mapped sides with winding 1 and 2 on original St42 face 21, St100 face 977 and St53 face 20. Cr107 face 358 has winding −1 and 0. The three distinct ray directions agree on each side. For comparison, exact arithmetic restored ordinary 0/1 sides at Cr121 face 405 despite an apparent floating-point anomaly there.

A separate libigl/CGAL cell selection compared positive, nonzero and odd winding on all twenty digital meshes. Twelve had a returned negative or multiple-winding cell. Boolean selection uses the arrangement of the floating-point source coordinates. Exported vertices and volume/inertia integrations are floating point. The nested-cube positive/nonzero and odd volumes were 64 and 56, compared with a signed volume of 72. Across the twenty archives the largest relative spread in material volume was 6.956×10⁻⁸ and the largest absolute inertia-ratio spread was 2.762×10⁻⁸, both on St18. Every digital convention matched the source's calculated ratios within 0.000723. These small differences establish close bulk properties for the three digital conventions. The study has no evidence that the discrepancies materially affected real printed orientation.

Fix a finite outer box Ωᴿ and impose the affine potential U·x on its boundary. Let Eᴿ(B) be the minimum of ∫|∇φ|² over fluid outside an insulating solid B in that box (without the kinetic-energy factor 1/2). Then Eᴿ(∅)−Eᴿ(B) = Uᵀ[Mᴿ(B)+|B|I]U, where Mᴿ is the perturbation-energy tensor for that **same outer boundary**. Restricting an admissible potential to a smaller fluid domain gives the finite-box ordering under inclusion of solids. The unbounded added-mass tensor M used above is obtained only in the expanding-domain limit. A finite-box computation would need bounds on both its field error and its difference from that limit before it could certify an unbounded tensor or its directions. Here the released masses screen aggregate print quality, and the inertia ratios derive from geometry. Neither supplies independently known inner and outer manufactured solids or certified fluid-field errors. All 128 angle records remain unscored.

## Reproduction

Run `python replay.py` from this directory with NumPy, SciPy and Shapely installed, or supply the original Nottingham ZIP with `--archive FILE`. The default receiver authenticates the original archive, reconstructs the metadata roster and volume moments, checks both frozen tensor banks, reproduces the exact winding witnesses and independently bounds the ellipsoid examples. The two preregistrations and original geometry records are included. `verify_orientation.py` additionally uses mpmath for the Carlson integral check. The digital-fill comparator uses libigl 2.6.3 with CGAL. Original scientific runtime versions were NumPy 1.26.4, SciPy 1.11.4 and Shapely 2.0.6.
