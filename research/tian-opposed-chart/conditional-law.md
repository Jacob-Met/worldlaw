# Opposed singleton illumination of the Tian resolution chart

The 2014 experiment reports 293 singleton exposures and 293 four-LED exposures of one chart. The author-linked resolution-target folder has one subdirectory containing 293 distinct numbered TIFF files. The chart parameter script enumerates the circular singleton lattice. Neither the directory nor that script identifies a second stack or gives the realized random pattern for each composite. Three sampled TIFF directories have one 2,560 × 2,160 unsigned 16-bit plane, with no subsequent directory or acquisition metadata. This leaves the proposed composite transfer unscored. A list of randomized patterns generated today could not establish the physical assignments.

For a separate test, the released sequential convention maps naturally sorted image numbers to the column-major entries of the chart LED mask. Reversing both lattice coordinates exchanges images j and 294−j, leaving image 147 fixed. There is no contemporaneous per-exposure control log in the observed release. Evidence for the assignment comes from the naming/mask and published reader convention. Mechanical LED alignment and equality of lamp output remain unmeasured.

Let the thin transmittance be t(r)=exp(iφ)a(r), with a real, and let the pupil obey P(−u)=conj(P(u)) up to a global phase. Under opposed unit plane waves, T_{−q}(u) is the conjugate reflection of T_q(u) up to that phase. Multiplication by P preserves this relation, so the image-plane fields form a conjugate pair up to a phase and their irradiances agree. Unequal incident fluence multiplies a frame by a positive scalar. Independently centering and taking the Euclidean unit vector of each measured spatial intensity field removes positive global gain and a single constant background. Spatial gain or offset maps, nonlinear camera response, complex transmittance, real alignment error and an incompatible pupil can alter the measured relation. A good prediction cannot identify which assumptions hold. A spatially fixed detector pattern can also increase measured similarity across many lamp orientations, so the direction-blind and geometry-informed controls matter.

The objective magnification measured in the chart script is 8.1485 with sensor pitch 6.5 µm and LED-to-sample separation 67,500 µm. At the central 256 × 256 crop the maximum nominal offset from center is bounded by 144.40 µm. The map from lateral LED displacement to transverse direction cosine is 1/z-Lipschitz. Centered opposed lattice sites therefore have direction-cosine sum of magnitude at most 0.00428 across that crop if the declared camera and illumination centers coincide. This bounds angular mismatch for the nominal geometry. It does not bound intensity mismatch without further information on the pupil and specimen.

The preread assignment covers all 146 opposed pairs. Forty-eight complete pairs are available for development. In each of the remaining 98 pairs precisely one end is available as a source frame. The target side has never been used as a calibration input or scale reference. All four noncentral brightfield pair directions fall in the 98-pair target group under the existing seeded assignment. Their opposite donors and the central frame are visible to development, but no complete brightfield pair trains a direction-dependent correction. The primary score retains all pairs. The two brightfield and darkfield strata are reported separately according to the nominal source NA, with no repeated-chart or measured-fluence claim.
