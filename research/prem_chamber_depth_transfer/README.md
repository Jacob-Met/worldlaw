A ray collected along the surface normal gave a less accurate forecast than transferring the measured change in emissivity from the calibration powder to either held preparation. ## Measurements and access

Prem et al. (2022) deposited three sheets of ambient and simulated-lunar-environment spectra. We used the 568-channel plagioclase sheet because Shirley and Glotch (2019) documented a nominal 0° emission angle for its PARSEC apparatus. The other minerals came from different measurement sources. The reference fraction was 32–63 μm. The other two were 63–90 μm and <32 μm. The model uses the paper’s respective nominal grain diameters of 47.5, 76.5 and 16 μm. Each native wavelength from 6.69926977 to 25.0501003 μm was retained. The original PARSEC publication reports 256 scans integrated into one spectrum at 2 cm⁻¹ resolution. We compare the deposited processed effective emissivity.

PARSEC used a bottom heater set to 350 ± 0.5 K, a tunable overhead lamp, vacuum below 10⁻⁵ mbar and a shield below 150 K. The acquisition used a stabilized spectral maximum and aimed for a brightness temperature near 350 K. These quantities do not measure the temperature profile in the powder. The deposited model’s typical 400 K deep temperature is a modeling choice. Its source publication discusses qualitative trends and historical fits for these fractions. Those reports were exposed before this archival numerical evaluation. The deposited workbook contains all columns in the same file. The predictor converted to numbers only the three wavelength grids, the three ambient spectra and the reference fraction’s SLE column. Two independent producer processes sealed all 44 predictions at commit 53570c90aeee76f3b9c08427839828f4208fd621 before the score process interpreted either held SLE column. The original larger PARSEC acquisition archive returned HTTP 403 on the first workbook download. That original remains failed, with no enlargement of the held set.

## A directional control for a hemispheric model

The deposited ReBL C program sums every photon escaping the top of a plane-parallel slab. Volume emission is isotropic, the bottom is black and Lambertian, and the angular scattering law is reciprocal. For a layer of width dz and absorptivity a, normalized hemispheric exitance gives the layer a coefficient 4 a dz times its probability of escape after a uniform isotropic emission. Reversing the paths, this coefficient is the probability that a photon incident at the surface with Lambertian-distributed direction is absorbed in that layer. The bottom coefficient is the probability of reaching the bottom. A ray launched exactly along the surface normal gives the central-ray alternative. The angular-response note derives this distinction. The finite 15° cone is a sensitivity check. The actual angular transmission of the instrument pupil is unavailable.

Before the physical spectra were used, 9.18 million forward emissions in the released C transport loops (single-process MPI identities, smaller synthetic workload, fixed seed) were compared with two reverse operators. Every hemispheric layer and substrate probability on six test media was within 3.990 estimated standard errors of the forward simulation, below the fixed limit of seven. On a pure absorber, independent analytic normal and hemispheric solutions agreed within 2.626 and 1.463 standard errors. These checks qualify the implementation over those examples. They cannot establish which operator predicts the chamber.

The physical calculation uses the released complex principal indices and Maetzler Mie formulas. A simplex grid of orientation fractions in steps of 0.01 was fitted to each preparation’s own ambient spectrum, with the author’s equal-orientation correction. All angles use these same fitted optical coefficients. Volume packing is fixed at 0.37, spheres scatter independently and the 100-layer slab extends 1 mm above a black bottom. The volume packing, actual sizes within each sieve and shape of each grain are potential discrepancies. The historical workflow names an automated smoothing operation without specifying it. All physical roles here use the same three-bin smoothing of brightness temperature before normalizing by the Planck radiance at the maximum smoothed brightness temperature. A wavelengthwise ambient/model correction is applied separately for each angle. The author-style 400 K control uses the deposited equations with this explicit numerical realization. The result does not reproduce every historical run or establish that the detector followed an exact central ray.

All gradients were selected only from the middle fraction. The primary normal operator used the source-selected hemispheric gradient unchanged. The two registrations also included separately fitted gradients, a cross-assignment of gradients, geometry scaling of the gradient depth, and two empirical predictions transferring the measured calibration change as an addition or ratio. No role adjusted a held scale, gradient or reference wavelength. For each seed and held fraction the primary had to beat the matched hemispheric fit, the 400 K hemispheric fit, unchanged ambient, measured difference and measured ratio.
