# Angular sampling and the released thermal transport equation

The header of emspec_mc.c in the 2022 ReBL release sets 100 layers of 10 μm each, 916 wavelengths and 100,000 bundles per source layer and wavelength. It emits uniformly in solid angle throughout a volume layer and with a Lambert law at the black lower boundary. Every upward bundle crossing the top is counted. This gives hemispherically integrated exitance. Shirley and Glotch (2019, section 2, DOI 10.1029/2018JE005533) assign 0° emission angle to PARSEC spectra. The angular extent and transmission of the spectrometer pupil have not been established. The forward operator with a ray exactly along the surface normal is a distinct central-ray approximation.

For laterally homogeneous layers, reciprocal scattering, no in-medium gain and a black lower boundary, reverse photon paths sample the same loss functional. Let a be the absorption coefficient, dz the layer thickness and P_i the fraction of isotropic uniformly distributed emissions from layer i reaching the upper surface. Normalize exitance by π times the black spectral radiance. The hemisphere coefficient is 4 a dz P_i. The bottom coefficient is its escape fraction under Lambertian emission. These factors follow by exchanging source and detector along a path under the reciprocal angular scattering density, integrating over volume emission (4π a dz) and dividing surface flux by π. Equivalently, launch a reverse photon at the surface with inward cosine distributed as sqrt(U); then the two coefficients are respectively the probability that the photon is absorbed in layer i, and the probability of reaching the bottom. A top-return path is unabsorbed and contributes zero. All probabilities are nonnegative and sum to one when top return and the bottom are included. Weight layers by B_lambda(T_i) and the bottom by B_lambda(T_bottom). For an ideal normal radiance observation the reverse cosine is one. A uniformly accepted circular cone requires projected-solid-angle weighting; no aperture width can be identified from nominal 0° alone.

The derivation applies to the fixed sample model, not to exact experimental temperatures, optical constants or detector alignment. The C reverse implementation uses the deposited one-degree angular probability bins and the same scattering, attenuation, free-surface and black-bottom convention. The eliminated repeated per-layer emission gives many depth coefficients in one multinomial simulation. Its statistical errors are correlated across depth. A full production comparison must report the grid, seed, simulation counts, CPU time and cold receiving. The six synthetic-media test changes workload constants and the clock seed in the released C; the original emission, transport and output loops are compiled unaltered, with MPI primitives replaced by their serial identities. It also compares normal and hemispheric layer masses to their distinct closed pure-absorption solutions. The tested tolerances assess implementation agreement and supply no measured evidence about which angular operator predicts PARSEC.

The published optical constants determine scattering and absorption only once particle shape, grain diameter, packing, and orientation weights are specified. The released orientation fitting example uses ambient spectra; one must exclude every held simulated-lunar-environment measurement from parameter selection. The source workflow further reports an unnamed automated smoothing function before its effective-emissivity normalization.
