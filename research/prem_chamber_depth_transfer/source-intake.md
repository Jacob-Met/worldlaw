# Source qualification for chamber mineral emission

The primary source is Prem et al. (2022), DOI 10.3847/PSJ/ac7ced. The official v1 release (Zenodo 6757550) contains one laboratory-spectra workbook, a workflow diagram and the model/code archive. The workbook is opened only by the metadata reader at this stage. It verifies the full deposited file MD5 and obtains sheet names, cell dimensions, text fields and source citations. The reader streams worksheet XML and emits no numeric spectrum cells. Emissivity arrays, fitted temperature parameters and numerical anisothermal spectra are outside this source admission.

The physical observable sought is a measured vacuum-chamber emission spectrum for a distinct prepared mineral fraction, conditional on the available ambient emissivity of that fraction and source calibration in other fractions. Published plots, conclusions, model parameter ranges and qualitative grain-size trends are prior exposure. The study cannot become a blinded replication of those published claims. Sampling many wavelengths within a single spectrum does not create replicate preparations.

In a medium with fixed optical response, idealized radiance obeys L(lambda)=integral K(lambda,z) B(lambda,T(z)) dz. Ambient measurements at a known constant temperature determine only each kernel's integral, and become relative shape measurements if divided by a brightness-temperature blackbody or normalized to a spectral feature. Equal-mass kernels at different depths have identical isothermal exitance and can give different spectra under a gradient. Measured ambient emissivity alone therefore does not determine a held-vacuum spectrum. Independently supported scattering/absorption coefficients or a preregistered published transport model are needed to predict spectral differences. Their calibration, temperature dependence, packing and channel normalization will be recorded.

The source paper constructs a wavelength-dependent modeled-to-measured isothermal correction and applies it to anisothermal calculations. Any matched baseline must receive the same ambient reference, calibration spectra, grain information, packing and allowed instrument normalization as a new prediction. Qualify how each acquisition was heated, shielded and referenced, whether each mineral's grain fractions shared an apparatus, and whether a target-derived surface brightness temperature or Christiansen reference enters the released effective emissivity. Effective-emissivity shape can be predicted as a self-normalized observable only under an explicit common operator for forecast and measurement. Fitting a held gradient, taking a held temperature as an input without attribution, or fitting any wavelength warp after viewing targets would change the information contract.

The finite transfer to consider after source qualification is a held material-size preparation, with externally fixed temperature-gradient depth or a depth law estimated solely from other measured preparations. Compare common geometric-depth, grain-depth and the paper's authentic temperature-gradient family, simple measured difference/ratio, and no change. Use independent-run metadata to determine the actual units and possible grouping before defining a course. The actual temperature field remains unobserved if the source supplies only numerical model settings. This step does not assume existence of a qualified comparison.
The original PARSEC methods (Shirley & Glotch 2019, DOI 10.1029/2018JE005533) report sample heating from below to 350 ± 0.5 K, adjustable lamp illumination from above, chamber pressure below 10^-5 mbar, and cold shield below 150 K. They let the spectral maximum stabilize and tuned the setup to a brightness temperature near 350 K. These are apparatus and acquisition conditions. Neither the shield temperature nor the brightness temperature establishes the subsurface emission-weighted profile. The 400 K deep temperature used by Prem et al. in their models is a different assumption. The three prepared labradorite fractions discussed in the paper (<32, 32–63 and 63–90 μm) came from this measurement study. The source screen will verify which traces and time/size labels are present in the deposited workbook. No held target is designated by inspecting its measurements.
