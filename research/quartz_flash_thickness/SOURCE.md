# Source boundary before inspecting the detector curves

Breuer and Schilling's release contains quartz laser-flash records from a Netzsch LFA 457. The primary source is DOI 10.35097/1215. The associated paper is DOI 10.5194/ejm-33-23-2021. The publisher identifies the 40,327,680-byte TAR by MD5 df726a12287749ee051eb65d7091a8b9. The native download returned SHA-256 cc29c01b1ef3ccfa90b56490f768d69a2dcdddcef4cde72d523e23c042cdd4fe. We used the public download path and checked the bytes before opening any member. The dataset carries CC BY-NC-SA 4.0. Its original detector records remain with KIT.

The paper describes six platelets cut from one synthetic crystal, 10 mm square faces, micrometer thickness measurements, a cut direction verified within 1 degree, and gold and graphite coatings. The measurement reports the rear detector's voltage against time after a front-face laser pulse. The high-temperature measurements use an InSb detector and the low-temperature measurements use HgCdTe. The published mean diffusivities usually use three shots at each temperature. These are repeated shots on a specimen. An independent specimen count requires the source specimen identity. At high temperature, orientation-dependent thermal expansion affects the thickness. A nominally common furnace temperature alone does not establish an identical source pulse or measurement chain.

Prior literature exposure includes the reported thick-cube directional crossover in the transition region, a published room-temperature diffusivity example, the paper's qualitative diffusivity trend and reported aggregate axial diffusivities near and above the phase transition. The acquisition metadata study did not inspect the published fitted tables or numerical detector-voltage series. The release gives both a time column and a voltage column.

The first native program used a broad inclusion expression for header fields. It stored the instrument's Half_time/ms, Pulse_max, Time_end and Time_final fields along with settings for 543 recognized specimen curves, and printed one example from each of nine groups. This included the first room-temperature HT2 record on each axis. The program did not convert voltage array cells to numbers. It skipped the HT1 and cube exports at their first unfamiliar header. These facts rule out describing all header-derived outcomes as unopened. The second reader uses an explicit list of acquisition keys, reads time values only, and records the first encoding refusal for each family. A third reader recognizes the interleaved zero bytes in UTF-16 exports and counts matches by progressively stricter same-temperature, sensor, flash drive, filter and gain criteria. Every original source is retained.

For homogeneous one-dimensional conduction with reflecting rear boundary, a front-face heat flux maps to rear temperature through 1/(k q sinh(qL)), q=sqrt(s/alpha). After normalizing by the adiabatic temperature rise, the impulse response of the **time derivative** has transfer H_L=qL/sinh(qL). If pulse and detector response are common, the thick-to-thin ratio for thickness b>a is R=(b/a)sinh(qa)/sinh(qb). The impulse response of R is a positive unit-mass delay distribution: Euler's product factors R into p+(1-p)/(1+s b²/(alpha pi² n²)), p=a²/b². Relative to the thin response it adds mean delay (b²-a²)/(6 alpha) and variance (b⁴-a⁴)/(90 alpha²). The corresponding consistently integrated, baseline-corrected rises obey the same convolution. It does not follow that the raw, unnormalized rear voltage, the trigger pulse, the instrument's reported half-time or any finite time-window normalization obeys the derivative formula. Optical penetration, radiative transfer, losses, changes in detector dynamics and finite aperture are distinct alternatives. The authors fit a Parker transport term, surface loss, temperature-dependent radiative components and a finite-pulse correction. Parameters fitted to a target trace would consume target information.

The exposure boundary and original file identifiers above were recorded before fitting any source waveforms or opening any target detector series.
