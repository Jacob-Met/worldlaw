# Prospective source qualification for circular-tank free-surface motion

The University of Plymouth deposit (DOI 10.24382/87c2f31a-524d-41ec-95d4-383fa82b10b8) advertises three water–air campaigns in a horizontal circular cylinder. The JFM paper (10.1017/jfm.2025.16) reports a 400-mm diameter, 40-mm-depth tank, a 60-Hz fixed camera, and sidewall and centerline surface displacements extracted after tracking the moving container. The ordinary vertical campaign targets the first *symmetric* mode around twice its eigenfrequency. Horizontal excitation does not automatically calibrate that symmetric mode. The published softening, period tripling, qualitative differences across fill levels, aggregate figures and the named example configurations are prior information. The numerical archive records remain unopened at registration.

The first audit should identify individual physical runs, any retained transient versus steady-state samples, measured versus nominal actuator motion, camera-derived elevation timing and synchronization, physical fill and uncertainty, and whether the released rows are individual repeats or aggregate plot data. A single full-surface contour and antinodes measured in the same image are not independent instruments. The main question is whether finite forcing and the actual geometry permit a prediction beyond a matched frequency/fill interpolation baseline on unseen released runs. No comparison may mix mode symmetries, equate a region of linear instability with a finite-amplitude response, infer a seed from a measured target amplitude, or relabel numerical outputs as measured targets. The Geometric or Floquet model would require a separately frozen empirical competitor, nuisance/timing treatment, and explicit treatment of unsupported curves. If only summary plots are archived, the physical estimand must explicitly use those released summaries or stop.

A distinct conditional response, if the export authenticates it, is to predict mean **centerline half peak-to-trough amplitude** from measured left/right amplitudes at the same forcing and fill. Linear potential flow gives |e_center/e_wall| for the first symmetric eigenfunction at a given fill. This prediction uses wall amplitudes at the target condition. It is not an initial-rest, forcing-only prediction. Both sidewalls and the center originate in the same camera frames. A symmetric free-surface mode has antisymmetric wall-versus-center motion in time; its half ranges discard that sign. The first eigenfrequency alone does not identify the wall-to-center ratio unless the physical operator/boundary conditions and eigenfunction are fixed. The source paper (sections 3.2–3.6) defines each amplitude as half peak-to-trough. It reports three repeats on only 16 of 123 cases, 350 excitation cycles before 30-s steady recording, and overlapping fill/drive comparisons at h/D=.30 and 2 mm. Figures or worksheets replotting the same runs cannot become independent calibration and held cases. The published 0.3-fill examples are exposed. The original paper derives the two-fluid integral eigenvalue formulation (Nyström) and forced Mathieu equation, so neither is a new law. Finite-amplitude mode mixing, menisci and camera boundary localization can defeat a modal amplitude ratio even where its eigenfrequency is correct.
