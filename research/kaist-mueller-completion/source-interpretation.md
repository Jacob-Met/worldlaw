# Interpreting an incomplete polarization measurement

The retained input is the linear 3-by-3 block of a previously estimated Mueller matrix. The target VV coefficient describes how incoming circular polarization contributes to outgoing circular polarization. Each original matrix used 36 retarder exposures and each reported angular bin took a median of ten jittered lookups. Predicting VV assesses completion of the published matrix. It does not reconstruct ten independent measured intensities or recreate a linear-only acquisition.

Let A contain the I,Q,U rows and columns, and normalize the matrix by a positive M00. For a nonsingular deterministic Jones system, the Lorentz identity gives a repeated generalized eigenvalue Δ of Aᵀ diag(1,−1,−1) A relative to diag(1,−1,−1), with Δ equal to the squared magnitude of the determinant of the normalized Jones operator. The VV cofactor gives det(A)/Δ. Changing from a Jones operator to its complex conjugate leaves A and VV unchanged and reverses the signs of the six linear/circular couplings. No single-frequency observation in A distinguishes that conjugate pair. The experiment concerns VV only.

Singular systems present a stronger ambiguity. Normalize the Jones operators |R⟩⟨R| and |R⟩⟨L| by their respective Mueller M00. Their linear blocks both equal diag(1,0,0). Their VV entries equal +1 and −1. Thus even the assumption of a single deterministic Jones system does not fix VV on this singular stratum. Convex mixtures of these systems fill the entire interval [−1,1].

More generally, a physical mixture of Jones systems has a positive semidefinite coherency matrix. Conjugation, followed by averaging the original mixture and its conjugate, preserves A and VV and cancels the six mixed circular coefficients. The feasible VV values are therefore the scalar interval for which the resulting real 4-by-4 coherency matrix is positive semidefinite. The same real-matrix construction underlies Gil and colleagues’ published maximal-purity criterion. They select the physically allowed VV of largest magnitude. When opposite extrema tie, neither sign has priority from that criterion. The frozen implementation records ties and assigns a zero fallback for scoring. Empty intervals and singular/noisy pure-system refusals also get declared fallbacks so difficult measured rows remain represented.

Baek and colleagues use right-handed incoming and outgoing frames whose vertical axes lie in the scattering plane. The released finite frame samples reverse both transverse axes relative to the literal prose definition, which rotates the transverse axes by π and leaves the Stokes coordinates unchanged. On these two frames a diagonal Fresnel Jones map has equal UU and VV. Adding a purely unpolarized diffuse term preserves this equality. The signed UU coefficient is therefore a same-information control with a direct physical interpretation.
