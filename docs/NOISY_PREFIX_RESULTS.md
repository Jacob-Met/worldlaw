# Fixed-prefix initialization: original registered results

## Decision

The initial-state estimator improved the registered primary arm from **125 to 130 correct admissions out of 132**, on the same acquired records and the same 112-time suffix. Six cases improved and one previously correct admission was lost.

The strict primary gate **failed**. The new contender and random arms both reached 130, and the contender still admitted one incorrect law. This result does not qualify active superiority, unrestricted SINDy, physical transfer, or the parent discovery oracle. The estimator remains an explicit option; earlier defaults and historical #893 results are preserved.

## Complete paired results

All 132 in-roster cases and 12 hidden-dynamics controls were retained. All arms use the same declared 17-sample initialization prefix and score indices 17–128. The old initializer is scored over that identical suffix.

| Random weak | 127 | 130 | 2 / 2 | 3 / 0 | 3 / 0 |
| Passive finite-template differential | 120 | 124 | 7 / 7 | 5 / 1 | 5 / 1 |

The committee's 131 versus random's 130 is a disclosed comparator result. It is not a post-outcome replacement for the registered primary. The passive differential method uses the existing two-coefficient finite law dictionary; it is not unrestricted SINDy.

Every old and new arm had zero admitted time-permuted shams across 144 cases. After the shared bounded twin-reset veto, every arm had zero admissions among the 12 outside controls. The reset witness contradicted 11 outside cases and none of the 132 genuine Markov cases. The remaining outside case was rejected by the forecast. Before this veto, the three forced arms each admitted three outside cases; the passive arm admitted three with the old initializer and four with the new initializer. Prefix fitting is therefore not an OOD certificate.

No actual-record prefix initialization failed in this course. The result is an observed paired gain on this fixed synthetic cohort, without a claim of general or statistically independent superiority across families, worlds or SNRs.
