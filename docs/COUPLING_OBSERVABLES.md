# What an integrated measurement can hide

In Coupling Lab, run a closed-path comparison and choose **Inspect responses
hidden by integrated work**. The inspection retains the same original training
and loop input. It compares each fitted field with every supplied torque component,
then places those findings beside its signed-work result. Work can remain
not-separated because of cancellation or because declared reading/integration
allowances are broad; the combined status does not assert which cause applies. It never fits the loop
readings, changes training ranks or interprets their evidence as independent.

The new observable is the individual vector response. For a fixed fitted model,
each component's residual is compared with its declared torque-error radius plus
an allowance for the two matrix products, bias addition and residual subtraction.
A component outside this allowance contradicts that conditional fixed prediction.
The strongest excess retains the exact source row, axis, paired angles, supplied
reading, prediction, signed residual and total allowance. Inside the allowance is
**not-separated**, not physical validation. Fitted-coefficient uncertainty and
input-angle uncertainty remain outside this comparison.

## A discriminating counterexample

An explicitly authored independent-field control on the retained unit-square loop
receives both independent and reciprocal fitted laws under integrated work. Yet
the reciprocal field misses six individual coordinate readings under ±0.01 N·m
bounds; the largest residual is approximately 1.0437317 N·m. Enlarging the reading
bounds to ±10 N·m removes that discrimination. These are software controls, not
newly acquired measurements or blind field tests.

The reason is structural. Every exact symmetric affine torque matrix defines a
conservative affine field. Its signed integral around any closed polygon is zero
in exact arithmetic. The inspector identifies competitive pairs whose matrices
are exactly symmetric; changing the shape or area of a closed-work experiment
cannot separate those fixed fields. This statement does not identify passive
hardware, account for latent state, or prove that observed physical work is zero.
The maintained polygon engine still retains its numerical intervals.

## Compare a different question

The inspector reuses the maintained `planCoupling` result from the **retained
training**. In the default controlled case, its permitted point [−1, −1] rad
separates the independent/reciprocal vector forecasts by approximately 1.439103
N·m even though all their exact closed-work forecasts coincide. This input is
outside the training coordinate ranges and remains labeled accordingly.

The suggestion is a bounded numerical comparison among already permitted states.
It is not new hardware authorization, an operation on the active notebook,
calibrated information gain or an automatic preference for point measurements.
No loop value or receiving-error radius enters the proposal. The ordinary
Coupling Lab controls remain the route for separately authorized acquisition.

## Source and lifecycle

The existing input-only circulation file retains everything needed to recompute
this inspection; no new saved verdict or notebook schema is added. Editing the
loop, loading another source or replacing active training removes the derived
inspection along with the parent comparison. Reopening a prior circulation file
uses that file's retained training. The active notebook remains separate.
