# Calibrating a whole-trajectory residual

The existing world-level residual calibration supplies the tie-preserving upper-tail count. This receiver connects it to raw, grouped full-state comparisons from the symbolic ODE/PDE simulator and performs the final threshold comparison in exact integer arithmetic. Its coordinate-pooled contrast below is an illustrative incorrect baseline; the established world-level API already retains one rank per independent world.

A separate execution first fixes one model, the numerical refinement tolerance, the acquisition definition, the measurement units and a risk level. The receiver scores each measured course with the existing full-trajectory simulator. One acquisition contributes the largest absolute residual across its complete windows, times and cells. This retains within-acquisition dependence and gives each acquisition one rank. Supply accurate, distinct acquisition and course IDs; repeated windows from one acquisition belong in the same group.

For m independent calibration acquisitions exchangeable with one future acquisition under the proposed model and measurement process, let S be its score and Sᵢ the m calibration scores. The returned upper-tail rank is p=(1 + count of i with Sᵢ ≥ S)/(m+1). With distinct scores each rank is equally likely under exchangeability. Ties can only increase this upper-tail p-value. Consequently the probability of a residual-outlier call is at most the declared α under those assumptions. The receiver compares rational α to this discrete rank by integer cross-multiplication. If 1/(m+1) exceeds α, it returns insufficient calibration resolution before scoring observations. A failed calibration or target numerical integration yields an explicit unresolved result. The API refuses overlapping declared acquisition/course IDs and puts a bound on total measured cells.

The test uses twenty authored courses generated by the correct stationary law. Each acquisition has two windows with a single nonzero sensor residual spread across 68 coordinates. Pooling coordinates gives a 95th percentile of zero and rejects 20/20 whole acquisitions. Leaving out each acquisition once and applying the grouped rank at α=1/20 rejects exactly 1/20. A new trajectory with acceleration 0.5 produces p=1/20, while a nonzero acceleration of 0.0001 remains inside the measured noise envelope at p=20/20. A tied boundary gives p=2/20. This example demonstrates the ranking and its resolution. Coverage on a different sensor, a post-selected model or the sealed campaign requires its own evidence.

Use `assessCalibratedTrajectory(model, calibration, target, {alpha, resolutionTolerance})` from `src/science/trajectory-residual-calibration.mjs`. Each acquisition is an object with a unique `id` and an array of `{course, observations}` records, in the same format as the original trajectory receiver. The integer numerator and denominator fields of `alpha` specify the exact risk level. A residual outlier is grounds to abstain from this model on this acquisition. Different physical causes can produce the same trajectory mismatch.

## Serial shape on previously computed forecasts

`assessForecastResidualShape(calibration, target, {forecastId, windows, alpha})` is an opt-in secondary check for forecast errors that cluster or alternate over time. The caller first freezes a model or forecasting procedure, a window layout and a risk level. Supply its predictions at the actual observation times on the untouched suffix. Exclude every fitted or imposed initial row. The receiver cannot establish the provenance of those predictions, convergence of their integrator or independence of the acquisitions.

Each acquisition supplies `{id, forecastId, records}`. Each window in the protocol has `{start, step, samples, channels}` with ordered channel names and a regular time grid. The corresponding record supplies `{id, start, step, channels, predicted, observed}`. Both arrays contain one numeric row per declared time and one column per declared channel. Channel units, coordinate conventions, identities and sampling policy must agree across acquisitions. Step and start use the caller's declared time unit and origin. Distinct records from the same acquisition belong to a single group.

Within one channel and record, the forecast error is the observed value minus the prediction in binary64. Assign each residual an integer rank equal to the number of smaller values minus the number of larger values. Equal residuals share a rank. For ranks r₀,…,rₙ₋₁, the serial score is |Σₜ₌₁ⁿ⁻¹ rₜrₜ₋₁| / Σₜ₌₀ⁿ⁻¹ rₜ². The numerator and denominator are exact integers after ranking the computed residuals. Each acquisition contributes its largest channel/window score, and integer cross-products compare these rational scores. The reported channel scores follow the protocol order. A constant channel has no serial information and receives null. An entirely constant record, residual overflow or fewer than four samples in a window precludes a result. Strong constant bias can still give a large amplitude error.

The upper-tail p-value uses one vote per independent acquisition, including ties. Under whole-acquisition exchangeability for this frozen procedure, the unconditional probability of calling a new acquisition an outlier is at most α. This bound is about false outlier calls. It supplies no bound on the chance that a wrong law passes. In particular, forecast errors sharing an estimated initial state can be serially correlated even with the correct law.

If both amplitude and serial shape will be tested, allocate their risks in advance and retain both results. To bound the probability of **either** false outlier call by 5%, equal Bonferroni allocation uses `{numerator:1,denominator:40}` for each endpoint and needs at least 39 independent calibration acquisitions. Two training worlds and several overlapping windows cannot supply that resolution. An unresolved endpoint leaves a combined admission unresolved. These functions do not automatically combine tests or turn a non-rejection into model identification.

The deterministic fixtures compare identical residual margins in different orders, keep disjoint records apart and exhaust every ordering of four distinct residuals. They exercise the calculation and its ties. Whether the statistic distinguishes unseen physical laws needs a separately registered acquisition study.
