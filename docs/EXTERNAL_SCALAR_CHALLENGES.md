# Keep a newly received scalar challenge outside fitting

The actual-file preview now offers a separate explicit action: **Challenge current laws without refitting**. It retains the original current competitive models and received source values, evaluates their predictions, and records a challenge-only notebook event. The ordinary append/refit action remains available and distinct.

A concrete authored control shows why this matters. Eight training rows follow torque=command. Eight later readings at command2 report torque2.6. The original law's receiving RMSE is.6N·m. Appending these readings changes the fitted gain to1.24 and reduces error on the reused outcomes to.12N·m. Challenge-only receiving preserves the original gain1 and.6error, with eight original training rows, unchanged model ranking and unchanged future suggestion. A smaller after-fit error is not fresh predictive validation.

Every challenge event keeps its source label/units, original reading rows, prior fitted models and complete per-model error summaries. The existing receipt area shows the original training-order alternatives and lazy selected-model rows; it never adopts a receiving-error winner or presents an after-fit comparison. Later manual refits or additional measurements do not rewrite that earlier snapshot. Notebook export saves original inputs only; reopening reconstructs the challenge at its chronological source prefix. Undo removes challenge evidence without removing training rows. Challenge rows never enter the original held-out set, training source, prospective planning, parameter search or later historical training prefixes.

Admission reuses the batch file and observation validators. Virtual instrument notebooks refuse external challenges until a real observation notebook is opened. Source/ratio unit declarations are validated; legacy measurement behavior is unchanged. Source provenance, independence and prior knowledge remain user declarations, not authenticated preregistration. Ordinary/robust/weighted fitted models can be evaluated without changing their estimator; raw torque error is not a calibrated acceptance test.
