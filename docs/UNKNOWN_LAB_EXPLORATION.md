# UNKNOWN LAB: an open-ended research direction within WORLDLAW

This document expands WORLDLAW's existing scientific ambition. It is not a new product repository, a replacement application, or a claim that the capabilities below are implemented. Preserve the maintained workbench, current owners and qualified experiments while developing new possibilities.


## Purpose

Help people discover how to investigate something they do not yet understand.

An unfamiliar phenomenon often arrives without the right description. The useful variables may be hidden, the instrument may distort the observation, several explanations may fit the available data, or the question itself may assume something false. UNKNOWN LAB begins with the possibility of an experimental collaborator that can help discover those missing pieces—not merely fit coefficients or write an articulate explanation after seeing the answer.

Its work should lead to executable ideas, discriminating observations, useful experiments and better predictions. It could help a researcher escape a misleading formulation, let a student investigate an unfamiliar system rather than repeat a known demonstration, or help an inventor understand why a prototype behaves unexpectedly. These are starting possibilities, not a final definition of its audience or scientific reach.

## What the experience could feel like

You present a small physical mechanism with a response nobody on the team can explain. Several descriptions fit the initial motion: a nonlinear restoring force, friction, an instrument delay, or an internal state left by previous actions. UNKNOWN LAB makes their disagreements visible. Instead of immediately declaring a winner, it asks what you can change or measure, and proposes an observation whose possible outcomes would change the explanation.

You might ask, “Why would repeating the same input tell us anything?” The system shows that one model predicts an identical response while another predicts dependence on what happened just before. Or it notices that a second camera angle would resolve an apparent motion better than another force measurement. The next valuable step can be a new observable or measurement arrangement, not just another sample in the existing table.

The actual result arrives and surprises every candidate. Rather than quietly changing the story, the workbench exposes what failed. Perhaps position and velocity were insufficient: a useful model needs internal state. Perhaps the system was changing during the experiment, or the sensor had a timing offset. You can explore those possibilities, compare new predictions, and investigate what distinguishes them. An important discovery might be that the original question cannot be answered with the available instrument.

Later, you encounter a different system. A representation or experimental strategy learned earlier might help, while the original equation does not. UNKNOWN LAB should make that distinction useful: what transfers, what must be remeasured, and what fails under the new conditions? The experience grows through contact with unfamiliar phenomena, not simply through a larger catalog of explanations it can recite.

The interface could make models, instruments, interventions and observations directly manipulable. A person could move between raw evidence, an explanatory diagram, predicted motion, residual structure and an executable model. Conversation can help formulate questions, but the actual experimental world—not a transcript or generic monitoring dashboard—remains central. Better interaction ideas are welcome.

## Illustrative capabilities

- Develop competing executable explanations rather than collapse immediately to one plausible story.
- Discover useful coordinates, latent state, observables, symmetries, conserved quantities or dimensionless relationships when the supplied representation is inadequate.
- Explore different kinds of model: equations, dynamical systems, causal structures, mechanisms, programs, or representations that emerge during research. No single family is prescribed.
- Propose experiments that distinguish explanations, including changes in measurement, intervention order, precision or sampling—not only points from a fixed action grid.
- Recognize when uncertainty comes from measurement, insufficient excitation, structural ambiguity, changing conditions, or a missing explanation.
- Compare predicted consequences with actual observations, retain meaningful failures, and revise both model structure and the questions being investigated.
- Connect ideas from literature and previous experiments to executable tests. Retrieved claims can inspire hypotheses; they do not count as newly discovered evidence.
- Help design or select instruments and simulated environments that reveal something otherwise inaccessible, while keeping simulated and physical results distinguishable.
- Transfer a useful abstraction or investigative strategy to an unfamiliar setting and discover where its assumptions stop working.

These are examples of abilities worth exploring, not a completion checklist. Unexpected discoveries may change which capabilities are most useful.

## The hard core

The challenge is not simply generating more hypotheses. It is finding representations and actions that make the world more understandable. A low-error fit may describe the data while missing the mechanism. Two incompatible models may remain indistinguishable until the experiment changes. A sophisticated model can absorb a sensor defect and become less scientifically useful than a simpler one. An apparently stable law may depend on a narrow regime that nobody has tested.

How can the system notice that its variables are wrong? When is it worth introducing hidden state, a new instrument, or a different abstraction? How can an experimental procedure be learned and reused without smuggling in knowledge of the answer? What makes a model explanatory and useful beyond the observations that produced it? How should a limited experimental budget be spent when every current model might be misspecified?

These questions invite substantial scientific and engineering invention. Methods should be chosen and challenged through actual experiments, not fixed by this seed. Human researchers should be able to inspect and redirect the reasoning; consequential physical experiments still depend on the authority and conditions of the real setting.

Progress could look like an experiment that separates previously convincing explanations, an unforeseen observation that leads to a better representation, or a transferred strategy that reduces the measurements needed to understand another system. Independent predictions, unfamiliar conditions, repeated measurements and comparisons at matched experimental budgets can reveal whether the system is learning to investigate or merely telling a better retrospective story. No one successful demonstration settles the larger frontier.

## Existing lineage and a genuine expansion

UNKNOWN LAB continues WORLDLAW rather than pretending to originate an unrelated idea. WORLDLAW's existing canon already calls for structural physical discovery, active experiments, falsification and transfer. Its working implementation provides a useful foundation: at source `18219203b22bbca59cc03463514138305bfb6dd7`, the maintained workbench searches subsets of seven scalar torque terms, fits coefficients, proposes states where competitive models disagree, receives observations, revises models and evaluates withheld predictions. Motion Lab connects explicitly qualified video tracking and telemetry to that workbench.

The meaningful expansion is not renaming that loop. It is investigating how a system discovers representations, missing model ingredients, questions, measurements and experimental procedures that were not supplied in advance. Existing solvers, notebooks, observation adapters, tests and receiving work should be examined and reused where they fit. Their present limits are research opportunities, not reasons to discard working machinery or claim general science from narrow results.

## And more

This could grow into new ways of learning science, building instruments, diagnosing unfamiliar systems, designing experiments, or collaborating with other scientific tools and people. It may reveal valuable questions outside the examples above. Models learned in one place may become instruments of discovery somewhere else; failed explanations may teach better questions; better observations may change what the system is capable of representing.

The product's direction should be able to evolve with those discoveries. The seed is an ambition to improve how understanding is created through interaction with the world—not a fixed terminal scope, a particular architecture, or a prescribed sequence of milestones.
