# One or two observations at the least declared effort

The UNKNOWN LAB probe instrument offers **Choose one or two offered states** alongside its existing single-state and two-state modes. The combined choice compares every offered singleton and unordered pair using one common numerical rule. It maximizes the number of separated surviving hypothesis pairs, then minimizes the declared effort. If both measures tie, it prefers fewer observations and finally a deterministic identifier order.

This is a prospective manual experiment request. It does not acquire a measurement or change the existing request-to-observation receiver.

## Use the existing instrument

Start the documented local WORLDLAW server and open `/unknown-lab.html`. Load a qualified cross-probe training study, offer the states and effort costs you can investigate, select the combined mode, and compare the offers. The table retains informative, unresolved and over-budget options. The selected option identifies whether one or two observations are needed, their combined effort, and the hypothesis pairs they separate.

The declared future contrast radius, fixed preparation, recovery and command remain part of the assumptions. Costs use your declared units; they are not estimated acquisition times. Changing the study, offers, budget, radius or mode invalidates the old export. Reopening a combined request restores its input records and recomputes the comparison.

The original single-state mode and pair-only mode retain their existing behavior and request schemas.

## Why a combined choice helps

An informative single state can separate the same hypotheses as an expensive two-state course. On the authored ambiguous training control, an offered zero-rate state with effort 2 and an offered reversed-rate state with effort 4 each separate the surviving rate and sine responses. The pair costs 6 without separating an additional pair of hypotheses. The combined policy therefore selects the effort-2 singleton.

A pair can also provide information that neither state supplies alone. The maintained joint-only control offers angle `asin(0.5)`, rates 0.47 and 0.53, effort 1 each, and contrast radius 0.001. Its singleton prediction sets overlap, while their complete simultaneous predictions cannot overlap with one shared coefficient per law. The combined policy retains that two-state option. An insufficient budget leaves it visible and unaffordable.

These are authored numerical controls. They do not establish physical acquisition cost or identify a real mechanism.

## One comparison policy for both sizes

The combined mode reuses the maintained exact half-plane comparator for both singleton and paired options. Every law retains its complete training-admitted coefficient interval and one coefficient throughout each proposed course. A closed overlap remains unresolved, including exact touching.

This differs deliberately from the original single-state interval screen's small numerical margin. Existing modes are unchanged. The new mode does not pool scores produced by different numerical policies. Its joint-only explanation also uses the new exact singleton comparisons.

The arithmetic is exact over the supplied binary64 feature values and coefficient endpoints. Inherited training screens, sine and hyperbolic-tangent evaluations, assumed experimental radii and physical feasibility are not upgraded to certified bounds. Pairwise discrimination does not establish a uniquely identified mechanism, and all offered models can be wrong.

## Complete bounded choice and replay

The mode accepts one to eight distinct offered states, giving at most 36 options. All singleton and paired comparisons share one 200,000-step geometry work budget. Work or arithmetic exhaustion throws without returning a partial ranking. Candidate evaluation and identifier ties use a deterministic ASCII order. Ordinary finite binary64 addition determines the summed declared effort; zero effort is allowed.

The `worldlaw.probe-choice/v1` export contains only its schema, validated training and acquisition settings. Import checks the bounded request and recomputes the model admission, predictions, complete options and selection. Supplied derived fields do not influence the result. The existing source-role checks reject challenge-bearing training input.
